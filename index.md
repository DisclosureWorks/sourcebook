# DisclosureWorks Sourcebook

Edition label: `2026-11`

**What the DisclosureWorks Sourcebook is.** A free, citable reference built from primary sources. In each entry's main text, statements drawn from a source carry a footnote you can check yourself, and cited sources are retained with their fingerprints. It is built with [Parallax](https://disclosureworks.ai/parallax/), a general research tool. The November 2026 edition's 102 short, plain-English pages cover how Congress oversees the intelligence agencies and the executive branch, the documentary and legal record of the Jeffrey Epstein case, chosen as the demonstration subject because its released record is public and large, and published accounts of intelligence operations. Later editions will add subjects unrelated to that case.

**The problem this solves.** Public-record research into government power is usually built on secondary reporting rather than primary documents, makes claims without evidence a reader can check, does not show how its conclusions were reached, and has no way to hold a large evidence set together over time. An investigation can release millions of pages; no reader can keep every claim anchored to the page that supports it, or see when a later release changes what an earlier one meant.

**Who it is for.** Congressional staff, journalists, researchers and lawyers who need the record rather than commentary, and need to cite it.

**How it is made.**

- **Primary sources first.** Pages are built from statutes, court filings, congressional reports and transcripts, declassified government records and released case files. Books and scholarship are cited for context, and the Historical Precursors area is labelled as a summary of secondary sources.
- **Citations checked.** Footnotes point to sources we hold. Before publication, quoted passages are checked against those sources, changes are reviewed by independent reviewers, and the publisher approves every page by name. Sentences that are our own analysis are approved as analysis.
- **Built with AI tools, approved by people.** AI research and review tools help draft pages, check citations against their sources, and run the independent reviews; nothing is published until the publisher approves it by name.
- **Fixed editions.** Each edition is frozen and fingerprinted, so a citation always points to the exact text you read. Corrections are made in public, never by quietly rewriting a page.
- **The evidence is kept.** The sources cited on our pages are retained with their fingerprints, so a claim can be traced to its document in any later edition and can be re-checked when new records are released.

**Where it is going.** The Sourcebook is updated every month with a new edition. Publications can be updated when there is new work. Corrections and feedback are welcome now at `corrections@disclosureworks.ai`. Future releases will open the DisclosureWorks Sourcebook to public collaboration and contributions.

**Get notified.** To hear about new editions, follow our [web feed](/feed.xml) or watch this site's [GitHub repository](https://github.com/DisclosureWorks/sourcebook) for releases. No sign-up is needed.
