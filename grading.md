# How we grade conclusions

Visible edition label: `2026-11`

Sourcebook entries distinguish what documents report from conclusions we draw.
A page has one of two status lines:

- **Reference.** *Reports what the sources say. States no conclusion of ours.*
- **Assessed.** *This page states a conclusion. Our assessment is below.*

There is no pending status. A page with our own conclusion must have a completed
assessment before publication. Reporting an author's attributed judgment does not
make that judgment our conclusion.

## Probability and confidence

We use the National Intelligence Estimate (NIE) Words of Estimative Probability
(WEP) vocabulary. These are approximate probability bands, not measured frequencies
or promises of certainty. The bands follow the Office of the Director of National
Intelligence (ODNI), [Intelligence Community Directive (ICD) 203, Analytic Standards](https://www.dni.gov/files/documents/ICD/ICD-203.pdf),
signed January 2, 2015, as amended. The labels below are the exact labels used in
our assessment schema; INSUFFICIENT BASIS is our unexamined-claim category and has
no probability band.

| Label | Approximate probability |
|---|---|
| ALMOST CERTAINLY | 95–99% |
| VERY LIKELY | 80–95% |
| LIKELY | 55–80% |
| EVEN CHANCE | 45–55% |
| UNLIKELY | 20–45% |
| VERY UNLIKELY | 5–20% |
| REMOTE | 1–5% |
| INSUFFICIENT BASIS | N/A (not applicable) |

Confidence describes the quality of the evidence and reasoning separately from
probability. **High** means strong sourcing and reasoning, not certainty.
**Medium** means credible sourcing with gaps or incomplete corroboration.
**Low** means fragmented evidence, weak corroboration, or uncertain reliability.
A low-probability conclusion can have high confidence when strong evidence weighs
against it.

## Evidence base

| Source tier | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `primary-multiple` | At least two independent primary records, such as sworn testimony, court records, official reports, declassified files, or on-the-record admissions |
| `primary-single` | One primary source |
| `secondary` | Scholarship, books, or institutional analysis drawing on primary material |
| `journalistic` | News reporting or investigative journalism, including unnamed sources |
| `speculative` | Inference, pattern matching, or an unsourced assertion |

Corroboration is **independent** when sources have separate origins; **partial**
when some support is independent but origins may overlap; **single-source** when
no corroboration is available; and **contradicted** when credible evidence opposes
the claim. Repeating one account across several publications is not independent
corroboration.

Our evidence rules require primary-single or stronger sourcing and at least
partial corroboration for LIKELY. VERY LIKELY requires primary-multiple and at
least partial corroboration. ALMOST CERTAINLY requires primary-multiple and
independent corroboration. Secondary sources alone cannot support LIKELY or
higher. Evidence floors do not automatically award a grade: the reasoning still
has to justify it. A strong label cannot conceal a weak evidence base.

Each block states one central conclusion, its evidence basis, and the assessment
date. At LIKELY or higher it also names a specific observation that would revise
the conclusion, the strongest counterargument, and our response. “More evidence”
is not a checkable revision condition.

## When a grade changes

A changed grade requires an Assessment Change Record explaining the old and new
judgments, the evidence or reasoning that changed them, and what would change
them again. A snapshot preserves the assessment state. Edition release notes
list every changed grade and link its change record. Changes to source tier,
corroboration, confidence, or conclusion also require a change record and snapshot.
An issued edition remains fixed; later editions and corrections preserve the
history so readers can see what we knew and why our judgment changed.
