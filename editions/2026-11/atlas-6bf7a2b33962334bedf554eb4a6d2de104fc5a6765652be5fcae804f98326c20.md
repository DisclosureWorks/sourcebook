# NSDD-286 (1987)

**Reference.** *Reports what the sources say. States no conclusion of ours.*

Edition label: `2026-11`

> Not everything on this page is footnoted. Our citation check covers the main text, but not headings, the lists of related entries and sources, or open questions, which may state facts without a footnote. Check the footnoted text or the source before relying on them.

**Definition:** **National Security Decision Directive 286**, issued by President <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Ronald Reagan</span> in 1987 establishing executive-branch procedures for the approval and reporting of covert actions.[^1] The directive was issued in response to the Iran-Contra disclosures and the Tower Commission report; Bruemmer reported in 1992 that the document was classified, with certain portions declassified.[^2] Per [Bruemmer (1992)](https://openyls.law.yale.edu/entities/publication/f24d637a-5286-41ea-8701-e0ca63f4bc44) (n. 21, citing Bruemmer & Silverberg 1988 for the unclassified portions): NSDD-286 is the executive-branch internal-procedural framework that the [IAA-1991](atlas-01ddbdf46d2d12ac962e38e57c964bec9fb88d63ec48c12d04fd0604805944e3.md)'s new Section 503 of the National Security Act "was largely a codification of."[^3]

## Overview

NSDD-286 was issued in 1987[^4] — the year of the Tower Commission Report (February 1987),[^24] the SSCI Preliminary Inquiry (February 2, 1987)[^5], and the Iran-Contra Joint Congressional Investigating Committees Report (November 1987).[^6] It was the Reagan administration's internal-procedural response to the Iran-Contra disclosures, establishing executive-branch procedures for:[^26]

- Presidential approval of covert actions (Findings)[^7]
- Reporting of covert actions to congressional intelligence committees[^8]
- DCI role in notifying Congress of special activities, including a right to be fully and currently informed of special activities conducted by agencies other than the CIA[^9]
- The directive prohibits the National Security Advisor and National Security Council staff from conducting special activities; McCormick & Smith identified a reporting gap for staff operations under the earlier statute.[^10]

Bruemmer reported in 1992 that the full document was classified, with certain portions declassified.[^22] The unclassified portions are described in <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Russell Bruemmer</span> & Marshall H. Silverberg, "The Impact of the Iran-Contra Matter on Congressional Oversight of the CIA," 11 Hous. J. Int'l L. 219 (1988), pp. 235–36.[^27]

## Historical significance

The Reagan-era sequence ran from the Iran-Contra disclosures (November 1986),[^11] after which [McCormick & Smith (1987)](https://doi.org/10.2307/419267) identified reporting ambiguities in the Intelligence Oversight Act of 1980 under three questions (who must report, what must be reported, and when),[^25] through the Tower Commission (February 1987) and NSDD-286 (1987), which set executive-branch procedures internally[^12] following consultations with the Senate Intelligence Committee,[^23] to the Intelligence Authorization Act of 1991, whose Section 503 Bruemmer describes as "largely a codification of executive branch procedures established through National Security Decision Directive (NSDD) 286, issued by President Reagan in 1987" ([Bruemmer 1992](https://openyls.law.yale.edu/entities/publication/f24d637a-5286-41ea-8701-e0ca63f4bc44) n. 21).[^13]

Much of NSDD-286 was developed in close consultation with the Senate Intelligence Committee and many of its provisions appeared in the Senate bill S. 1721.[^28]

The Ford-era sequence, as this page arranges events recounted by Olmsted (1996) and Kitts (1996):[^30]
1. Crisis (Hersh NYT story, December 1974)[^21]
2. Containment commission (<span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Rockefeller Commission</span>, January–June 1975)[^14]
3. Internal procedural framework (EO 11905, February 1976) — Ford's three executive bodies for intelligence budgeting, operational review, and oversight[^15]
4. (No statutory codification in the Ford case — Charter Legislation failed, partly per Bruemmer 1992 because the Carter administration ended up opposing efforts to codify many provisions of its own intelligence executive order)[^16]

Establishing a comparison of the Ford-era cycle with NSDD-286 and the IAA-1991 as a recurring [executive-branch counter-mobilization](atlas-ba0864ef2c3382b1d49623dabc1f304edce8f13c791c4c7842eca567dd150e12.md) pattern requires evidence beyond the cited directives themselves.

**Bruemmer’s largely-a-codification characterization** also bears on the Haas (2023) typology: Haas places IAA-1991 in the **procedural-prohibitive** quadrant (statutory limits on executive procedure),[^17] but Bruemmer describes the IAA-1991's procedural limits as largely a codification of the executive's own NSDD-286 procedures.[^18] Haas herself notes that the IAA, though classified as a prohibitive, procedural law, also codified practices commonly observed by the president and the intelligence community and preserved the status quo;[^29] whether statutes that codify executive-internal procedures work in practice as procedural-enabling remains an open question.

## Open Questions

- What is the publication provenance of the unclassified NSDD-286 extract associated with the Federation of American Scientists?

- Which requirements in IAA-1991 Section 503 went beyond the procedures in the unclassified NSDD-286 extract?[^19]
- How did NSDD-286’s restriction on NSC staff participation in special activities compare with the 1991 statutory reporting requirements?[^20]
- Is NSDD-286 the canonical primary-source instance of executive-branch counter-mobilization in the Reagan era, following the reform-recapture pattern that Olmsted (1996) and Kitts (1996) describe for the Ford era?
- Did NSDD-286 and the IAA-1991 amount to a Reagan-era move toward statutory codification of executive-internal procedures comparable to the Ford-era cycle?
- Is Bruemmer (1992) the primary scholarly account of the relationship between NSDD-286 and the IAA-1991?
- Was William Webster DCI from 1987 to 1991, and did he operationalize NSDD-286 across the Iran-Contra cleanup period?
