# Suicide Watch Protocols

**Reference.** *Reports what the sources say. States no conclusion of ours.*

Edition label: `2026-11`

> Not everything on this page is footnoted. Our citation check covers the main text, but not headings, the lists of related entries and sources, or open questions, which may state facts without a footnote. Check the footnoted text or the source before relying on them.

**Context:** Federal Bureau of Prisons (BOP) protocols for monitoring high-risk detainees at Metropolitan Correctional Center (MCC)[^1]  
**Relevance:** Multiple simultaneous protocol failures directly preceded <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Jeffrey Epstein</span>'s death[^2]

## Standard Protocol

The OIG report describes distinct requirements rather than one universal suicide-watch protocol: suicide watch involved direct continuous observation[^3]; twice-hourly rounds and institutional counts were general SHU duties[^4]; and the post-observation cellmate direction named Epstein, although an MCC New York local policy also required that inmates discharged from suicide watch be assigned a cellmate:[^5]

- Continuous or frequent observation (step-down levels)[^6]
- Cellmate assignment when removed from formal suicide watch[^7]
- Documented SHU rounds at least twice each hour, once in each half-hour, on an irregular schedule no more than 40 minutes apart[^8]
- Institutional population counts[^9]

## Failures in Epstein Case

The following failures were all in effect on the night of August 9–10, 2019, though their onset was staggered over the preceding weeks:[^10]

1. **Removed from suicide watch** — after the unresolved July 23 incident, which officials treated as a possible suicide attempt, Epstein was stepped down from formal suicide watch[^11]
2. **No cellmate assigned** — a written July 30 Psychology Department direction required Epstein to have an appropriate cellmate after observation ended[^12]; no replacement cellmate was assigned after his cellmate was transferred out on August 9[^13]
3. **Required SHU rounds not performed** — <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Tova Noel</span> and <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Michael Thomas</span> failed to conduct checks[^14]
4. **Falsified logs** — OIG found that both guards falsified records to indicate checks had been made[^15]
5. **DVR not recording** — see [DVR Failure (MCC Tier L Camera)](atlas-c6c36087a82eb0661e908f8fa2fb324a23b863415b576818a06c3bec3c3432d1.md); the camera covering his tier was live but not recording[^16]
6. **Institutional counts not done** — the regular institutional headcount was not performed[^17]

<span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">William Barr</span> described this as "a perfect storm of screwups"[^18]. On the missed checks, he said it was apparently a consistent practice; the OIG reported finding no evidence contradicting the FBI's determination of no criminality in how Epstein died.[^19]

## Institutional Response

Barr described his response to the death as follows:[^20]

- Appointed a new BOP Director and Deputy Director, Kathy Hawk Sawyer and Tom Kane[^27][^21][^23]
- Reassigned the MCC warden[^22]

Barr also recalled stopping new intake to MCC and believed he redirected it to Brooklyn MDC; he did not date that action.[^24]

Guards <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Noel</span> and <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Thomas</span> were indicted for falsifying records[^25]; both entered deferred prosecution agreements, and the charges were dismissed after they fulfilled the agreements[^26].
