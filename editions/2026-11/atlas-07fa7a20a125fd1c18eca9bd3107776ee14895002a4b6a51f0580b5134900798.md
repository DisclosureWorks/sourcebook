# Blue Pipeline

**Definition:** In *The Other Side of Deception*, Victor Ostrovsky calls an alleged Israeli arms-sales channel to Iran “Blue Pipeline.”[^1] Ostrovsky’s note links the nickname to a mistaken delivery in a light-blue Zim shipping container.[^20] The operational descriptions below, including pilot training, shipping, German intelligence contacts and police payments, are allegations in this memoir; neither independent corroboration nor continuation beyond 1986 is established by the cited passage.[^2]

## Source

[Ostrovsky, Victor. *The Other Side of Deception: A Rogue Agent Exposes the Mossad’s Secret Agenda*. First edition. New York: HarperCollins, 1994. ISBN 0-06-017635-0. Held electronic version.](https://archive.org/details/othersideofdecep00ostr), Chapter 19, PDF pp. 148–149: Ostrovsky recounts an airport conversation in which an interlocutor suspected that an insider wanted a proposed arms deal stopped because the deal interfered with Blue Pipeline.[^3] It was the deal, not the arrest, that allegedly interfered.[^3] On the same pages Ostrovsky writes that he himself had placed the call to the FBI, at his handler’s request, and gives a different purpose for it, so the interlocutor’s suspicion is one of two explanations within the memoir.[^3] The interlocutor’s identity and claimed intelligence-liaison role lack an authenticated primary record here; the personal identification and criminal implication are therefore omitted.

## Operational components per Ostrovsky

1. **Pilot training, in Ostrovsky’s account.**[^4] Ostrovsky describes flight training at two airfields in **Schleswig-Holstein** in specially modified Cessnas, and at a third location in five simulators brought from Israel.[^4] *"Israeli pilots were training Iranian pilots in several locations."*[^4]

2. **Parts for Iranian aircraft, in Ostrovsky’s account.**[^5] Ostrovsky writes that Iran’s air force consisted mainly of American-made fighter jets such as the Phantom, and that parts traveled overland from ports in Italy across Germany into Denmark, where they were loaded on Danish ships; other parts and weaponry were transferred directly from Israeli-leased ships to Danish ships in a Danish port.[^5]

3. **German intelligence contact:** Ostrovsky attributes the contact to the German Federal Intelligence Service (Bundesnachrichtendienst, BND) and says it was made at department-head level rather than through the upper echelons.[^6]

4. **Local police tolerance, as alleged by Ostrovsky.**[^7] "The local police authorities in the northern German state were well aware of the activity but had no qualms about it, as long as there were no terrorist activities in the area and the operation was pumping money into their personal pockets."[^7]

## Ostrovsky’s Interpretation of the Arms Case

In [Ostrovsky, Victor. *The Other Side of Deception: A Rogue Agent Exposes the Mossad’s Secret Agenda*. First edition. New York: HarperCollins, 1994. ISBN 0-06-017635-0. Held electronic version.](https://archive.org/details/othersideofdecep00ostr), Chapter 12, PDF p. 86, Ostrovsky separately speculates that the arms-case affair diverted attention from <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Amiram Nir</span>’s Iran dealings.[^8] This is the author’s inference, not an admission in the later airport conversation.[^8][^3] The Walsh report, Chapter 1, “United States v. Robert C. McFarlane,” dates the delegation’s Tehran arrival to May 25, 1986; it does not establish a simultaneous April mission or prove a cover operation.[^9]

## Significance

Ostrovsky describes an alleged channel with several forms of support for Iran.[^10] The following points summarize his account, without establishing its duration or an extension of Iran-Contra:[^10]

- **Duration:** Ostrovsky’s account describes an alleged channel in 1986; Chapter 19, PDF pp. 148–152, does not establish its subsequent duration.[^11]
- **Training and parts:** Ostrovsky alleges that Israeli pilots trained Iranian pilots in Germany and that aircraft parts traveled through Germany and Denmark.[^12]
- **German contacts:** Ostrovsky alleges participation by department-level German intelligence officials and says the operation enriched local police personally; the passage supplies no independent operational or investigative record.[^13]
