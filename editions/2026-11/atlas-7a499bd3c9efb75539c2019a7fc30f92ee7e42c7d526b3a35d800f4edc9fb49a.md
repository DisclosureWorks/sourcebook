# Southern Trust and DNA Mining

**Definition:** Southern Trust was an <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Epstein</span> company[^1] that Whitney Webb reports netted him $200 million.[^2] Epstein told the U.S. Virgin Islands Economic Development Commission (EDC) that it would offer genetic-data analysis services;[^3] the U.S. Virgin Islands later alleged in court that it appeared to have no clients and performed no visible informatics services.[^4] Webb frames the planned project as data-intelligence infrastructure.[^5]

## Overview

Whitney Webb (*One Nation Under Blackmail*, vol. 2, pp. 370–371) reports that Southern Trust sought to gather genetic data,[^6] build a search engine for disease links,[^7] and provide biomedical and financial consulting,[^8] and that the company netted Epstein $200 million in a few years.[^9] The source describes proceeds to Epstein, not funding he put into the project.[^10]

## Whitney Webb's Intelligence-Data Framing

She frames Southern Trust as a data-collection project with intelligence applications:[^11]

Potential applications of a comprehensive human DNA database raise distinct questions:

- **Identification:** DNA can identify individuals from trace materials, potentially helping identify an individual when suitable DNA profiles, comparison records and confirmatory testing are available.[^19]
- **Genealogical intelligence:** Appropriate genetic analyses can support inferences about biological relationships, genetic ancestry and possible risks for some health conditions.[^20]
- **Leverage:** If the database included samples from prominent individuals, the holder would possess highly sensitive personal medical/genetic information.[^21]

In Webb's framing, Southern Trust's plans parallel Carbyne911 and Chiliad.[^12] The cited account describes what the company sought or planned to do, not a demonstrated system operating at massive scale.[^13]

## Eugenics Interest

Epstein's eugenics interest is separately documented.[^14] Webb, citing press reports, writes that Epstein told friends and scientists he wanted to impregnate women at his Zorro Ranch[^15] and reportedly sought to "seed the human race with his DNA";[^16] Virginia Giuffre has alleged that Epstein and Maxwell asked her to serve as a surrogate.[^17] An August 2018 email also shows Bryan Bishop sending Epstein a funding plan Epstein had requested for a designer-baby and human-cloning company.[^18]
