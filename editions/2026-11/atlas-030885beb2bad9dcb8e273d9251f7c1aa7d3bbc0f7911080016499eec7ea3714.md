# Suspected Epstein Financial Offenses

**Reference.** *Reports what the sources say. States no conclusion of ours.*

Edition label: `2026-11`

> Not everything on this page is footnoted. Our citation check covers the main text, but not headings, the lists of related entries and sources, or open questions, which may state facts without a footnote. Check the footnoted text or the source before relying on them.

**Definition:** Suspected financial offenses—including money laundering and operating an unlicensed money-transmitting business—that congressional correspondence says SDFL investigated and a prosecutor recommended charging, but that were not adjudicated.[^1]

## Overview

Financial-crime allegations against Epstein were not charged under the NPA.[^2] Public reporting and congressional investigation have since sought to reconstruct the alleged conduct and the extent to which major financial institutions are alleged to have facilitated it.[^3]

## 2007 SDFL Money-Laundering Investigation

Per Bloomberg reporting (October 31, 2025), SDFL prosecutors conducted an **18-month money-laundering investigation** of Epstein between approximately 2007 and 2008:[^4]

- Found "at least tens of millions of dollars in questionable financial transactions."[^5]
- Documented a specific pattern: Epstein directed employees to withdraw "large amounts of cash to disburse to women around the world he was suspected of having victimized."[^6]
- The lead prosecutor recommended charging Epstein with **money laundering** and operating an **unlicensed money transmitting business.**[^7]
- Per Garcia's October 31, 2025 letter, these charges were never pursued because <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Acosta</span> elected to execute the NPA with Epstein.[^8]
- Reports cited in Garcia's letter indicate the investigation materials are likely in DOJ's possession; the letter says they must be disclosed under the Committee's subpoena to DOJ.[^9]

**Relevance to Acosta testimony:** Garcia's letter argued that reporting on an 18-month investigation called <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Acosta</span>'s claimed lack of recollection into question.[^10]

## Banks

Multiple major financial institutions managed Epstein's accounts;[^11] regulators found, and civil plaintiffs alleged, that they did so despite red flags of suspicious activity — in the New York State Department of Financial Services (NYDFS) consent order against Deutsche Bank,[^12] the U.S. Virgin Islands complaint against JPMorgan Chase[^13] and an October 2025 class-action complaint against Bank of America;[^14] for BNY Mellon the record cited here is congressional correspondence, not a pleading:[^15]

- **JPMorgan Chase** — Extensive role;[^16] subject of NYT investigation (Sept 8, 2025: "How JPMorgan Enabled the Crimes of Jeffrey Epstein").[^17] Former employer of Paul Morris, the banker Epstein followed to Deutsche Bank (per the Senate Finance Committee minority report; the NYDFS Consent Order calls him RELATIONSHIP MANAGER-1 and does not name his prior bank).[^18]
- **Deutsche Bank** — See detailed section below. $150M NYDFS penalty (2020);[^19] the court approved a separate $75 million civil survivor settlement.[^42]

Per reporting relayed by Garcia, 2007 SDFL prosecutors subpoenaed several financial institutions that served Epstein during the money-laundering investigation.[^24] Per reports cited in the letter, such evidence is likely in DOJ's possession.[^25]

## Deutsche Bank — NYDFS Consent Order (2020)

A detailed official account of one bank's relationship with Epstein is the July 6, 2020 NYDFS Consent Order against Deutsche Bank AG, Deutsche Bank AG New York Branch, and Deutsche Bank Trust Company of the Americas.[^26] Deutsche Bank maintained over 40 accounts for Epstein and associated entities from August 2013 through December 21, 2018 (terminated after the Miami Herald *Perversion of Justice* series).[^27]

**Onboarding approval:** According to the NYDFS consent order, Epstein was onboarded via a single approval email from EXECUTIVE-1 (unnamed senior executive) stating "[n]either suggest [that the Epstein relationship] requires rep risk and we can move ahead so long as nothing further is identified through KYC and AML client adoptions."[^28] The Bank's Americas Reputational Risk Committee (ARRC) did not meet in connection with Epstein's initial onboarding; it met about the relationship on January 30, 2015, and kept no minutes.[^29] Epstein was classified high-risk and "Honorary PEP"; the resulting enhanced monitoring was not tailored to the specific risks he posed.[^30]

**The Butterfly Trust:** Opened January 24, 2014.[^31] Beneficiaries included CO-CONSPIRATORS 1–3 and additional women with Eastern European surnames.[^32] Purpose stated as "pay all expenses/disbursements related to the trust."[^33] Through the Butterfly Trust account and other accounts, Epstein sent over 120 wires totaling $2.65M to trust beneficiaries; separately, his various accounts paid over $7M in what appear to have been settlement payments to law firms and over $6M in what appear to have been legal expenses for Epstein and co-conspirators.[^34]

**Cash withdrawals:** The order records 97 withdrawals of $7,500 each, the Bank's limit for third-party withdrawals ($727,500 in all), and separately says total cash withdrawals exceeded $800,000.[^35] After a two-day split, AML staff said the pattern appeared to be structuring; ATTORNEY-1 denied that intent.[^36]

**Termination and reference letters:** Terminated Epstein's accounts December 21, 2018.[^37] RELATIONSHIP MANAGER-2 then drafted reference letters to two other financial institutions on Deutsche Bank letterhead, one stating he was "unaware of any problems relating to the operation or use of [the] accounts."[^38]

**Penalty:** $150,000,000 to NYDFS, payable within 10 business days of July 6, 2020 signing.[^39] The Order's Paragraph 121 releases Deutsche Bank from further NYDFS action for "the Bank's relationship with Jeffrey Epstein and related individuals and entities," provided the Bank complies with the Order; Paragraph 122 preserves NYDFS action on Epstein-related conduct the Bank did not disclose.[^40]

**Note on penalty amounts:** The $150M figure is the regulatory penalty to NYDFS.[^41] The court approved a $75 million Deutsche Bank settlement with Epstein survivors.[^43] The court awarded class counsel 30% of the settlement amount ($22.5 million) plus $1,014,763.33 in expenses.[^45] These are distinct from the NYDFS Consent Order.[^44]
