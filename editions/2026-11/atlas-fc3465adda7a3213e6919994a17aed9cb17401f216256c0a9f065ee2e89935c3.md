# Iran-Contra Second Channel

**Reference.** *Reports what the sources say. States no conclusion of ours.*

Edition label: `2026-11`

> Not everything on this page is footnoted. Our citation check covers the main text, but not headings, the lists of related entries and sources, or open questions, which may state facts without a footnote. Check the footnoted text or the source before relying on them.

**Definition:** The Iranian-side intermediary relationship that replaced the discredited "First Channel" (<span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Manucher Ghorbanifar</span>) in August 1986[^1]; centered on a nephew of Iranian Speaker <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Hashemi Rafsanjani</span> ("the Nephew")[^2]; active through the November 1986 exposure that ended the Iran arms initiative.[^3] Called "the Relative" in the Tower Board's and the congressional committees' narratives.[^4]

## Overview

After Ghorbanifar failed multiple CIA polygraphs[^5] and the McFarlane–North May 1986 trip to Tehran ended without producing a hostage release[^6], <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">North</span>, <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Hakim</span>, and <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Secord</span> sought a new Iranian interlocutor with closer access to senior Iranian leadership.[^7] Rafsanjani's nephew became the principal Second Channel contact from August 1986.[^8] Hakim, who opened the channel, took part with Secord in the meetings with an Iranian emissary close to Rafsanjani from Brussels (August 1986) through Frankfurt and Mainz (October 1986)[^9]; in October North left Hakim to negotiate with the Iranians, and Hakim produced the nine-point plan.[^10]

Two features of the Second Channel, compared with the Ghorbanifar relationship:

1. **Closer Iranian leadership access.** The Second Channel Iranians were viewed as more promising intermediaries for releasing the hostages, though the committees concluded they represented the same principals as the First Channel.[^21]

2. **Hakim's commitments beyond presidential policy.** <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Hakim</span> extended North's earlier plan into the **nine-point proposal**, including a commitment to develop a plan to induce Kuwait to release the seventeen Da'wa prisoners (North's PROF-note version provides for "some," not all).[^11] The proposal went beyond stated U.S. policy[^12]; Poindexter testified that the President approved it, but Shultz testified that Reagan reacted with shock when told of it in December 1986, and Reagan then authorized Shultz to ignore "any unauthorized understandings"[^13]; [Walsh](https://archive.org/download/WalshReport/Walsh%20Report%20volume%201%20Investigations%20and%20Prosecutions.pdf) reported that North, <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Secord</span>, and Hakim had worked out the formal plan, and that the Iranians later insisted on adhering to it with George Cave's apparent agreement.[^14]

## Significance and evidence

The records this section cites:

- [Walsh Vol. 1](https://archive.org/download/WalshReport/Walsh%20Report%20volume%201%20Investigations%20and%20Prosecutions.pdf) documents the negotiation history and the nine-point proposal.[^15]
- Tower Report Part 3 funds-flow diagrams (Transaction 6, October 1986) attribute a $7M deposit into Lake Resources on September 26, 1986 to "Iranian representatives (the Second Channel)," with $2.037M transferred to a CIA Swiss account that had been closed[^16]; Walsh instead records a $3.6 million second-channel payment deposited on October 29, 1986 into the Enterprise's Hyde Park Square account.[^17]
- [Iran-Contra Congressional Report 1987](https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/SERIALSET-13810_00_00-002-0433-0000) narrates the Second Channel's role.[^18]
- The Culvahouse PIOB Submission Folder 5 (January 21, 1987) contains a released two-name list giving Bahramani's and <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Darvish</span>'s names, filed in the folder assembled for submission to the President's Intelligence Oversight Board.[^19]

## Open Questions

- Was the Second Channel a Rafsanjani-sanctioned strategic relationship for the Iranian leadership, or a factional intelligence-collection operation that the November 1986 Tehran leak ended?
- Was the Second Channel emissary (“the Nephew” or “the Relative”) <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Ali Hashemi Bahramani</span>, whose name appears in the Culvahouse two-name list?[^19]
- What role, if any, did <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Kamal Darvish</span>, named in the Culvahouse two-name list,[^19] play in the Second Channel?
- Was the two-name list in the Culvahouse submission intended to identify the Second Channel emissary and his associates?
- How did the Second Channel’s September and October 1986 payments relate to the diversion of arms-sale proceeds?
- Given the disputed testimony about presidential approval[^13] and Reagan’s later authorization for Shultz to ignore “any unauthorized understandings,”[^20] what commitments under Hakim’s nine-point proposal, including the Da’wa proposal, remained operative after the November 1986 exposure?
