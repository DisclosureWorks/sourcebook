# Arms-for-Hostages (Iran Initiative)

**Reference.** *Reports what the sources say. States no conclusion of ours.*

Edition label: `2026-11`

> Not everything on this page is footnoted. Our citation check covers the main text, but not headings, the lists of related entries and sources, or open questions, which may state facts without a footnote. Check the footnoted text or the source before relying on them.

**Definition:** The covert U.S.-Israel program (1985–1986) to sell arms to Iran in exchange for the release of American hostages held by Iranian-backed groups in Lebanon;[^1] Reagan publicly denied it was Arms-for-Hostages (Iran Initiative);[^2] <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Walsh</span>'s report concluded that Reagan "had convinced himself that he was not trading arms for hostages"[^3]

## Overview

The Iran arms initiative began in the summer of 1985 with an Israeli proposal, brokered by <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">David Kimche</span> and mediated by <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Manucher Ghorbanifar</span>, to sell U.S. weapons to Iran through Israeli intermediaries.[^4] The theory was that Iranian "moderates" could influence Lebanese hostage-takers to release American captives.[^5] Reagan authorized the initial Israeli sales without a written Finding[^6] — CIA General Counsel Stanley Sporkin drafted a retroactive Finding after the November 1985 HAWK shipment.[^7]

The initiative ran from August 1985 to November 1986 — approximately 15 months of arms sales that produced limited hostage releases,[^8] generated $28.6 million in receipts from sales of U.S. government weapons to Iran for the Enterprise,[^9] and created the financial surplus that <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">North</span> and <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Hakim</span> diverted to the Contras.[^10]

## Key Events

**August 20, 1985**: Israel shipped 96 TOW missiles to Iran; first Israeli delivery.[^11]

**September 14–15, 1985**: Israel shipped 408 more TOWs; 504 TOWs total.[^12]

**September 15, 1985**: Reverend Benjamin Weir released — the first (and at this stage only) hostage freed.[^13]

**November 1985 — HAWK fiasco**: 18 HAWK missiles shipped via CIA proprietary airline[^14]; Iranians rejected them — officials in Iran were upset that the wrong model of HAWKs had been delivered;[^15] Iran demanded return;[^16] CIA General Counsel Sporkin drafted a retroactive Finding.[^17]

**December 5, 1985**: Reagan signed a Finding authorizing covert action; it explicitly described the operation as Arms-for-Hostages (Iran Initiative).[^18] <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Poindexter</span> later destroyed this Finding[^19].

**January 7, 1986**: NSC meeting — Reagan, <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Bush</span>, <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Shultz</span>, <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Weinberger</span>, <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Casey</span>, <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Meese</span>, <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Regan</span>.[^20] Shultz and Weinberger opposed.[^21] Reagan approved continuation.[^22] The Finding explicitly instructed CIA not to brief Congress — which the committees' majority treated as a violation of the notification requirements (the minority called the withholding legal).[^23]

**January 17, 1986**: New Finding signed — direct U.S. sale to Iran, with Israel no longer the seller (an Israeli charter still flew the missiles on the last leg).[^24] The Finding directed the CIA Director not to notify Congress, and the "gang of eight" (the leaders of each party in the House and Senate, and the Chairmen and Ranking Minority Members of the Intelligence Committees) was never notified.[^25]

**From February 18, 1986**: Two shipments of 500 TOWs each, 1,000 total, were delivered to Iran —[^26] Southern Air Transport ferried the missiles from the United States to Israel, and an Israeli charter carried them on the last leg to Iran;[^27] <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Khashoggi</span> fronted $10M through Lake Resources;[^28] no hostages were released.[^29]

**April 1986 — Diversion Memorandum**: North's memo allocated $12 million of the residual funds from the planned arms transaction to the Contras — the "diversion"; the earlier $800K surplus came from the Israeli advance for the November 1985 HAWK shipment.[^30]

**May 25, 1986 — Tehran Mission**: <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">McFarlane</span>-led delegation failed;[^31] Iran offered to release two hostages immediately and two more after the HAWK parts were delivered;[^32] McFarlane refused and departed.[^33]

**July 26, 1986**: Father Lawrence Jenco released — partial gesture after the Tehran mission failure.[^34]

**July 29, 1986**: <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Amiram Nir</span> briefed VP Bush at the King David Hotel, Jerusalem, describing the initiative in detail.[^35] Craig Fuller took notes.[^36]

**August 1986 — Second Channel**: Ghorbanifar replaced by "the Nephew"[^37]; at the October 6-8, 1986 meeting with the Second Channel in Frankfurt, Hakim continued the negotiations and worked out a nine-point proposal that went beyond the plan advanced by North.[^38]

**November 3, 1986**: Lebanese magazine Al-Shiraa published story;[^39] Rafsanjani confirmed November 4.[^40]

**November 13, 1986**: Reagan gave public speech denying Arms-for-Hostages (Iran Initiative) —[^41] Walsh later concluded that Reagan had convinced himself he was not trading arms for hostages.[^42]

**November 25, 1986**: Meese announced diversion;[^43] Poindexter resigned;[^44] North fired.[^45]

## Walsh's Findings

The signed Finding explicitly characterized the initiative as Arms-for-Hostages (Iran Initiative), and the committees described arms transfers in exchange for hostage releases.[^46] Three hostages were released during the initiative (Weir, Jenco, Jacobsen); three more were taken (Cicippio, Reed, and Edward Tracy), netting zero improvement in the overall hostage situation.[^47]

## The Finding Problem

Reagan signed the January 17, 1986 Finding authorizing direct U.S. arms sales to Iran.[^48] The Finding explicitly instructed CIA not to brief Congress — which the committees' majority treated as a violation of the National Security Act's notification requirements (the minority called the withholding legal).[^49] The Finding directed the withholding of notice from the "gang of eight"; the committees' majority treated the withholding as a statutory violation, while the minority called it legal.[^50]

## Open Questions

- After the January 17, 1986 Finding directed the CIA not to notify Congress,[^25] was that direction ever rescinded, and if so when?
- What happened to the three additional American hostages taken during the initiative (Reed, Cicippio and Edward Tracy)? Were they eventually freed, and under what circumstances?
- Was the CIA proprietary airline that flew the November 1985 HAWK shipment St. Lucia Airways?
- Poindexter testified he destroyed the December 5, 1985 Finding because it was "politically embarrassing";[^51] did anyone else know of or authorize its destruction?
- Is the "Nephew"/"Relative" who replaced Ghorbanifar in August 1986 the same Ali Hashemi Bahramani named (with Kamal Darvish) in the January 21, 1987 Culvahouse PIOB submission?
