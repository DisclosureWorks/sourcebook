# President's Intelligence Oversight Board

**Reference.** *Reports what the sources say. States no conclusion of ours.*

Edition label: `2026-11`

> Not everything on this page is footnoted. Our citation check covers the main text, but not headings, the lists of related entries and sources, or open questions, which may state facts without a footnote. Check the footnoted text or the source before relying on them.

**Definition:** Executive-branch intelligence-oversight body created by Executive Order 11905[^1] (February 18, 1976, Ford administration)[^2] and re-established, under the name President's Intelligence Oversight Board, by EO 12334 (December 4, 1981, Reagan administration)[^29]; operates within the Executive Office of the President as a small panel of private citizens reviewing intelligence-community legality and propriety;[^3] reports findings to the President and (where appropriate) the Attorney General.[^4] Distinct from the President's Foreign Intelligence Advisory Board (PFIAB)[^5], which advises on intelligence-collection effectiveness rather than legality.[^30] Abbreviated **IOB** in contemporaneous executive-branch documents[^6] and **PIOB** in the Independent Counsel's 1993 final report.[^7]

## Overview

The IOB's mandate is to review intelligence activities for compliance with the Constitution, statutes, executive orders, and presidential directives; to review the propriety of activities that may not be illegal but raise oversight concerns; and to refer matters warranting prosecutorial attention to the Attorney General.[^8] Membership is small: three appointees under Executive Order 11905.[^9] Under that order it reported its findings to the Attorney General and the President at least quarterly; under the 1981 order it was to inform the President of intelligence activities that any member believed unlawful and forward to the Attorney General reports concerning intelligence activities it believed may be unlawful.[^10]

The Board operates within the White House Office and reports directly to the President.[^11] Executive Order 12334 requires security protection for information made available to the Board and binds its members, staff and consultants not to reveal classified information obtained through their service.[^31]

## Role in Iran-Contra oversight

The Board is the recipient of the **January 21, 1987 Iran/Arms Transaction Document Submission**[^12] (Wallison-era, retained in <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Culvahouse</span>'s files after his March 1987 arrival)[^13] — a five-folder package[^14] documenting the executive branch's record of the Iran arms initiative and contemporaneous National Security Council (NSC) working materials.[^15] Folder 5 of 5 (“Iran/Arms Transaction: Document Submission to President's Intelligence Oversight Board – 01/21/1987 (5).” Culvahouse, Arthur B.: Files, Box CFOA 1129. Ronald Reagan Presidential Library. Digitized folder, released 2018.) contains, alongside withdrawal sheets for nine NSC items withheld as national-security classified when processed in 2014 (four memos to John Poindexter, three of them from Oliver North, and five papers; the dated items fall between September 8 and 24, 1986),[^16] a typed two-name list for Ali Hashemi Bahramani and Kamal Darvish, annotated by hand with what appear to be building-entry notes (a gate, a private elevator, times and a name).[^17] Its presence in the folder shows the names were part of the package assembled for the oversight body; the page itself does not say it was prepared for, or sent to, the Board.[^18]

The PIOB submission is dated January 21, 1987,[^19] nearly five weeks (33 days) after Walsh's December 19 1986 appointment as Independent Counsel[^20] and five weeks before the Tower Report's February 26 1987 publication.[^21] The IOB's posture alongside these two investigations is itself a research question: how did the IOB's review interact with the Tower Commission's ongoing work and Walsh's prosecutorial investigation, and were the IOB's findings ever made available to either body?

Alongside the Tower Board and Attorney General Meese's inquiry, the IOB was one of the executive branch's internal reviews of Iran-Contra.[^28]
