# Project Babylon

**Reference.** *Reports what the sources say. States no conclusion of ours.*

Edition label: `2026-11`

**Definition:** Iraq’s supergun program, on which Canadian scientist <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Gerald Bull</span> was working;[^1] the Royal Armouries names him as its designer.[^2] Bull was killed in Brussels in March 1990; Ostrovsky alleges Mossad responsibility, which is not independently established here.[^3] British Customs seized supergun components, as recorded by the Royal Armouries.[^4] These facts do not establish when the entire program ended.

## The related Al-Abid launch

Ostrovsky describes Al-Abid as a three-stage Iraqi missile launched on December 5, 1989 and presented by Iraq as a satellite launcher.[^5] He alleges that Israeli intelligence considered the launch a failure but exaggerated it publicly.[^5] This is the memoir’s account, not an independently checked technical assessment.[^5]

Ostrovsky places the alleged exaggeration of Al-Abid within his account of [Operation Brush-Fire](atlas-7c61a63bbacdfc1efb017c2c6f84ea7e21fa492d3575575aaa8f7821f8f95a30.md), a psychological-warfare campaign.[^12] This attribution does not independently establish either the launch’s performance or its effect on Western policy.[^6]

## Ostrovsky’s account of the threats to Bull

Ostrovsky alleges that Mossad analysts expected Bull to continue his work despite threats and that his killing was intended to draw attention to Iraqi weapons.[^7] No contemporary profile or operational record independently supports this account here.[^7]

Ostrovsky, attributing the account to a source, alleges that two Mossad officials warned Bull and that a Kidon team later killed him.[^8] [Names and roles of alleged warning-delivery officials withheld.] The memoir itself says Belgian police had not solved the murder;[^8] the perpetrator is not independently established here. The memoir places the killing eight days after Bazoft’s execution,[^8] which Hansard records on March 15, 1990.[^9]

## Precursor: Israeli-South African G-5/G-6

James Adams describes South African development of the G5 from Space Research Corporation (SRC) technology and further development of the G6.[^10] Adams also writes that nearly all recent Armscor developments derive from Israeli products or from improvements on older Western material;[^10] neither statement confirms Ostrovsky’s claim that both guns were originally built in Israel by Sultam.[^11] The sources do not justify presenting the Sultam origin claim as settled fact.
