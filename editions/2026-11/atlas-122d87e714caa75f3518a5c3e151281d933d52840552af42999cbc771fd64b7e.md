# Operation Midnight Climax

**Definition:** A CIA MK-ULTRA activity in which CIA-funded safe houses in New York and San Francisco were used for unwitting drug administrations and observation.[^1] Sidney Gottlieb described prostitutes as among the people involved in the West Coast drug milieu; his testimony does not establish the same recruitment method at every site or that all sessions were filmed.[^2]

## Overview

Operation Midnight Climax was run by <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">George White</span>, a Federal Bureau of Narcotics agent working as a CIA consultant, under the MK-ULTRA program.[^3] White operated a short-lived New York arrangement before transferring west and ran the San Francisco safe house; the 1963 Inspector General report records a parallel East Coast arrangement established in 1961, and Gottlieb's deposition attributes a later New York safe house to Dr. Triechler and another supervisor without dating it.[^4] Some unwitting drug administrations were observed through one-way mirrors, but the cited testimony does not establish that every session was filmed.[^5]

The 1963 Inspector General recommended terminating unwitting domestic testing; a November 1963 leadership meeting recorded that testing had been held in abeyance and left any continuation decision with the DD/P, with possible referral to the Director.[^6] John Marks quotes a later letter from White to Gottlieb: "I was a very minor missionary, actually a heretic, but I toiled wholeheartedly in the vineyards because it was fun, fun, fun."[^44]

## Operational Details

- **Safe houses:** San Francisco; apartment in Greenwich Village, NYC[^7]
- **Method:** Gottlieb testified that some of White's informants were prostitutes; unwitting subjects were dosed without consent[^8]
- **Documentation:** Sessions were observed through one-way mirrors; Gottlieb recalled photographic surveillance, but his testimony does not establish that every session was filmed.[^9]
- **Funding:** CIA MK-ULTRA budget; White was simultaneously a Federal Bureau of Narcotics (FBN) supervisor[^10]
- **Duration:** CIA–White relationship ~1953/55–1963; San Francisco safe-house activity about three or four years (Gottlieb, hedged)[^11]
- **Oversight:** Formally under MK-ULTRA (<span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Sidney Gottlieb</span>, Technical Services Division)[^12]
- **Documentation:** Gottlieb discussed the Bureau of Narcotics safe houses in 1975 Church Committee testimony[^48]

The program is a documented historical example of CIA drug testing.[^13] Bryant, after discussing allegations tying Craig Spence to the CIA, cites Midnight Climax and asks whether American politicians were targeted by CIA spycraft;[^49] the cited primary records do not establish that Midnight Climax tradecraft was transmitted to Spence or Epstein.

## Church Committee Exposure

CIA Director <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Richard Helms</span> had ordered MK-ULTRA files destroyed in 1973.[^14] Sidney Gottlieb (MK-ULTRA chief) testified and was immunized.[^34]

## Primary-Source Detail from Gottlieb's 1983 Orlikow Deposition

The [May 17, 1983 sworn deposition](https://nsarchive.gwu.edu/document/32736-document-20-deposition-sidney-gottlieb-phd-civil-action-no-80-3163-mrs-david-orlikow) of <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Sidney Gottlieb</span> in *Mrs. David Orlikow et al. v. United States* documents Operation Midnight Climax in primary-source detail.[^15] Direct primary-source admissions[^50]:

- **Three safe houses**: New York City and San Francisco, of which Gottlieb had personal knowledge, **and Marin County**, which he "heard about" (work there on the **aerosol administration of LSD**).[^16] Gottlieb learned of the Marin work in 1962-63, after the fact, while either out of TSD or back as Research and Development Director.[^17]
- **Later NYC safe house (Avenue of the Americas, downtown Manhattan)**, set up by Triechler: small three-room apartment in a 5-to-8-story building.[^18] Gottlieb could not say where White's own New York administrations took place.[^35] Approximately 4-5 unwitting LSD administrations to White's "own contacts and informants" before White transferred to the West Coast.[^19] Reports were informal and oral; asked whether they were committed to writing, Gottlieb answered, "No, as far as I remember."[^33]
- **A second NYC safe house** was set up later by Triechler with another supervisor (not White), which operated for "a year or two"; Gottlieb was "not aware of any activity that took place in that."[^20]
- **SF unwitting LSD administration count**: Gottlieb "30 or 40" — explicitly contradicting other testimony alleging "more like 200" ("**that is a ridiculous figure**").[^21] All unwitting; no doctor present.[^22]
- **Photographic surveillance recalled** at SF safe house ("Yes. I have a remembrance of that being true"[^23]); possibly electronic surveillance.[^23]
- **No subject-protection instructions**: Gottlieb gave White no instructions: "He was only requested to report to us what happened after his LSD administration."[^24] Asked what instructions <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Lashbrook</span> gave White, Gottlieb replied: "I doubt whether he gave him any."[^25]
- **Bureau of Narcotics witting personnel**: White, <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Anslinger</span>, "**three other people**" — five officials in that answer.[^26] Gottlieb did not name the other three in that answer; he said he did not remember who they were.[^26]
- **Records destruction confirmed**: White's San Francisco written reports were "**specifically destroyed when the files were destroyed in '72, '73**."[^27] When plaintiff suggested some such documents had turned up since: Gottlieb directly denied — "**That is not true.**"[^28]
- **Characterization**: Operation Midnight Climax was "**a series of more or less simulated, as far as we were concerned, operational uses**" — Gottlieb himself disclaiming the "experiment" framing as "pushing the term quite a bit."[^29]
- **Termination ~1963**: Reason: "we knew the answers to what we need to know".[^30] Gottlieb did not remember the 1963 IG survey of TSD being directly related to the termination — though that IG Report had recommended termination of unwitting U.S. citizen testing.[^31]

## Subjects, Prostitutes, and the Drug Scene

Per the deposition (pp. 410-412), the involvement of prostitutes was a function of the operational MO White was simulating: "the involvement of prostitutes in the West Coast activity had to do with the MO, the modus operandi of this whole drug culture. They came in and out of it that way. I am sure some of his informants were prostitutes, some of his drug buys took place with their involvement…there was not a focus on prostitutes as a generic group of subjects but rather they were involved as they would be in the drug scene."[^32]
