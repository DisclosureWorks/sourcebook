# Maxwell's Whereabouts, July 2019 to July 2020

**Reference.** *Reports what the sources say. States no conclusion of ours.*

Edition label: `2026-11`

> Not everything on this page is footnoted. Our citation check covers the main text, but not headings, the lists of related entries and sources, or open questions, which may state facts without a footnote. Check the footnoted text or the source before relying on them.

**Definition:** The interval between <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Jeffrey Epstein</span>'s July 6, 2019 SDNY arrest[^1] and <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Ghislaine Maxwell</span>'s July 2, 2020 FBI arrest in Bradford, NH.[^32][^2] In mid-August 2019, Maxwell's whereabouts were largely unknown to the public, the press, and (per public reporting at the time) federal authorities, although an FBI situation report dated Aug. 15, 2019 listed Maxwell as a co-conspirator.[^3] The In-N-Out Burger photograph dated Aug. 12, 2019 in Universal City, Los Angeles is a widely published alleged sighting of Maxwell during the period;[^4] the image's provenance and possible manipulation remain disputed.[^5]

## Overview

After Epstein's death, press reporting suggested Maxwell could become a federal target.[^6] Per [NY Post](https://nypost.com/2019/08/15/jeffrey-epsteins-gal-pal-ghislaine-maxwell-spotted-at-in-n-out-burger-in-first-photos-since-his-death/) reporting published Aug. 15, 2019, AG <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">William Barr</span> stated on Aug 12, 2019: *"Any co-conspirators should not rest easy."*[^7] Maxwell had already left public view: the Post wrote that she "seemingly dropped from the face of the Earth when sex abuse allegations began to surface against her close pal," and the Daily Beast that she "vanished from public view after he was indicted";[^8] press speculation in the weeks after Epstein's death (Aug 10, 2019)[^9] placed her variously in the UK ("holed up in her British manor"),[^10] on the Massachusetts coast (<span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Scott Borgerson</span>'s Manchester-by-the-Sea, MA mansion),[^11] or abroad ("Is she in Europe?").[^12]

The In-N-Out Burger photograph dated Aug. 12, 2019 — published by the NY Post on Aug. 15 — is a widely published alleged sighting that punctuates this period, and its provenance is disputed (see below).[^13] Approximately 11 months later, on July 2, 2020, the FBI arrested Maxwell in Bradford, New Hampshire.[^33][^14]

## Evidence and Open Questions

The public record leaves several evidence questions open:

### Q1 — Provenance of the In-N-Out photo — DISPUTED

The [NY Post](https://nypost.com/2019/08/15/jeffrey-epsteins-gal-pal-ghislaine-maxwell-spotted-at-in-n-out-burger-in-first-photos-since-his-death/) originally framed the photograph as **"The Post found the socialite hiding in plain sight"** with a **"NO CREDIT"** photo annotation.[^15] The [Daily Beast](https://www.thedailybeast.com/photo-of-jeffrey-epstein-pal-ghislaine-maxwell-at-in-n-out-burger-was-staged-paper-claims/) later reported the Daily Mail's allegation that <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Leah Saffian</span> staged the image, while [Frank Report](https://frankreport.com/2019/08/19/source-california-uk-attorney-took-pictures-of-ghislaine-maxwell-for-ny-post-and-attorneys-dog-in-photo-too/) quoted an unnamed source alleging that Saffian took and sold it to the Post; neither allegation has been independently established.[^16]

**Reported indicators, which this page groups as one source cluster, plus a separate limited image analysis:**[^17][^18][^19][^20][^21]

1. **EXIF metadata** tagged **"Meadowgate"** — reported as matching a business associated with Saffian[^17]
2. **Dog identification:** The dog at Maxwell's feet is reported to be **Dexter**, Saffian's own dog, said to be prominently featured on Saffian's Facebook[^18]
3. **"Good Boys" billboard that Outfront Media's spokeswoman believed was Photoshopped:** **Outfront Media** (the outdoor-advertising agency that manages the bus shelter behind Maxwell in the photo) confirmed: *"We have no record of 'Good Boys' ever being posted"* at that location.[^19] The actual ad at that bus shelter since **July 28, 2019** was for **Providence St Joseph's Medical Centre**.[^20]
4. **Independent forensic analysis** by Adam Wandt (forensic photo expert, John Jay College of Criminal Justice) via Inside Edition: *"There's a wave of line going down her back that's indicative of Photoshop manipulation"* and *"There's also brush strokes visible at the bottom that would lead me to believe that somebody possibly added Miss Maxwell to this picture"*[^21]

The photographer told the Post that Maxwell was dining alone; a published image shows two meals (one on the table and one on a bench below) and two phones.[^22]

Published accounts offer competing, attributed provenance claims.[^38] The Daily Mail account, as summarized by the Daily Beast, alleged that Saffian staged the photograph; Frank Report quoted an unnamed source alleging that she took and sold it to the Post.[^23] The public record reviewed here does not independently establish those allegations or the Post's handler chain.

The Daily Beast reported in August 2019 that Saffian could not be reached for comment; she later returned a call from LA Magazine but said "I don't give interviews," and the public record reviewed here does not contain a Saffian statement on the staging allegation.[^24]

**Status:** Provenance remains unresolved. The Daily Beast, LA Magazine, and other stories repeat the Daily Mail allegation;[^25] [Inside Edition](https://www.insideedition.com/infamous-photos-ghislaine-maxwell-n-out-burger-show-signs-being-photoshopped-expert-says-55365) separately reports Adam Wandt's narrower opinion that somebody "possibly added" Maxwell to the image.[^26] The Daily Mail article was not directly available during review, leaving its underlying sourcing unverified.

### Q2 — Was the reappearance choreographed? — OPEN

The available evidence does not establish that the reappearance was choreographed. Several outlets repeated a Daily Mail allegation that the appearance was staged; a separate expert said parts of the image may have been manipulated.[^27] Those reports do not establish who staged or supplied the image, and the public provenance remains unresolved. The questions below describe hypotheses, not findings.

- **Why this venue (Universal City In-N-Out), if the appearance was staged?** — possible answers include geographic accessibility from the Encino law office of Saffian, whose company Meadowgate Media Investments Inc the EXIF tag is reported to match[^28], tabloid-friendly visual aesthetic (fast-food incongruity for a British socialite), and the bus-shelter background that the alleged edit overwrote with the *Good Boys* ad[^29]
- **Why the *Good Boys* film as the alleged overlay?**
- **Why this timing — two days after Epstein's death + the same day as Barr's "Any co-conspirators should not rest easy" statement?**[^31]
- **How did the photograph reach the NY Post?** — the record does not establish who supplied it (the EXIF indicator is reported, not confirmed) or who at the Post handled it.

### Q3 — Where was Maxwell between August 2019 and July 2, 2020?

In its December 2020 bail application, the defense said Maxwell moved into the New Hampshire house in December 2019 and stayed there continuously for approximately seven months until her arrest.[^39] A guard, <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Martin Anthony Jackson</span>, in an FBI report of his July 2, 2020 interview, said he arrived at the Bradford house on or about May 8, 2020, and that he was under the impression, from his conversations with Maxwell, that she had been there since before Christmas 2019 without interruption.[^40] The government's July 2020 detention memorandum alleged that she had been hiding in locations in New England and had moved locations at least twice; the Court's bail ruling on July 14, 2020 said she "moved to New England, changing locations on multiple occasions"; and the defense later answered that she was not "changing locations on multiple occasions".[^41][^63][^39] These accounts do not supply a complete itinerary for August to November 2019.[^39][^41]

An FBI report of a July 13, 2020 interview gives the account of a witness who showed the property.[^71] The report says the buyers used the names "Steve Marshall" and "Jen Marshall", that the witness met them at the property on November 2, 2019 and again on or about November 5, 2019, and that they made an offer on or about November 6, 2019.[^71][^72] According to the report, the buyers said they were "currently staying in Ascutney, Vermont"; the woman using the name Jen Marshall said she was looking for a residence with "ultra privacy"; the witness said the buyers "needed to set up an LLC" and used "their own law firm in Boston"; the buyers indicated they "would be paying cash"; and at the December 13, 2019 closing "the buyers did not show up".[^72] The report says that after the arrest the witness saw photos of Maxwell in the papers and "recognized" Maxwell as the woman using the name Jen Marshall.[^71] This is one witness's account as the FBI recorded it.[^71] An email with a November 27, 2019 sender line of "Steve Marshal", spelled that way, says "our Boston law firm Nutter will execute closing documents on behalf of the LLC as the manager".[^73] In one released copy of the closing file, the bank's notice of the December 12, 2019 wire reads "ATTN: JEN MARSHALL" under "Originator to Beneficiary Information"; another released copy of the same notice has the name blacked out.[^74]

The same report says the witness later identified the man who arrived first at the first showing as "Scott Burgerson", spelled that way, "from photos of same in the media".[^75] A note of a July 14, 2020 call with the FBI says agents showed the witness a photo of Borgerson.[^76] The two records differ on how the identification was made, and this page prefers neither.[^75][^76] Being at a viewing shows nothing about ownership or payment.

The Bradford closing file names Granite Reality LLC, acting through its manager, as buyer and gives December 13, 2019 as the closing date.[^42] The file's recording sheet and a December 10, 2019 broker email give a price of $1,070,750, which the email describes as a reduction; a draft account summary in the government production gives $1,070,800, and an FBI affidavit, which spells the name Granite Realty LLC, gives $1,070,000; these figures differ and are not reconciled here.[^42][^43][^44] The closing ledger records a $994,279.12 wire from the LLC and a $100,000 deposit listed under the real-estate agency, and the wire notice names Citizens Bank as the sending bank.[^45] In July 2020 the government wrote: "It is unknown whether the defendant purchased the property in cash, or whether she has a wealthy patron who did so on her behalf".[^46] These closing records do not establish the ultimate personal source of the money or the LLC's beneficial owner.[^42][^45]

New Hampshire's filings name three people as manager at different dates.[^77][^78][^80] A certificate of formation filed on November 18, 2019 and the 2020 annual report name Jeffrey W. Roberts, care of the Boston law firm Nutter McClennen & Fish LLP.[^77] A change filed on July 28, 2020, 26 days after the arrest, and the 2021 annual report name Leah Saffian.[^78] The state dissolved the company in September 2023 over unfiled annual reports or fees.[^79] A reinstatement and the annual reports for 2022, 2023 and 2024, all filed on September 16, 2024, name Scott Borgerson, so they do not show when he took over.[^80] He also signed the company's December 25, 2024 cancellation, which gives as its reason "House sold on 10/30/2024."[^81] These filings show who was listed as manager on those dates.[^77][^78][^80] They do not show why the manager changed, who owned or funded the company, or that any of these people knew of any conduct.[^77][^78][^80][^81]

The draft account summary said the relationship between Maxwell and the <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Montpelier Trust</span> "is unknown at this time" and inferred one, in part from the LLC's listed address, which it said matched the trust's apart from the ZIP code; it did not trace the house payment from the trust.[^43] A July 13, 2020 email from an FBI forensic accountant reported that the trust's account-opening documents listed Maxwell as grantor of an irrevocable trust established on July 31, 2018, and gave the trust's address of record at a Boston law firm, while calling its attached analysis a draft subject to change.[^47]

An April 2022 government email in the released files includes a chart showing about $7.95 million moving on November 12, 2019 from "Montpelier Trust and Corporate Services, Ltd" to a brokerage account under Scott Borgerson's name, and $1.2 million moving from that account to Granite Reality on December 11, 2019, two days before the closing.[^82] The email says "proceeds actually flowed to it's purchase."[^82] The chart is a government summary, not a bank record: the email says it was derived from the Financial Crimes Enforcement Network, it carries a printed Bank Secrecy Act warning against further dissemination, and another released copy of the email has the chart blacked out.[^82][^83] We did not locate the underlying transactions for the chart's arrows, though our searches read text and could miss a scanned record. The chart's first box lists Ghislaine Maxwell as grantor.[^82] A grantor is not necessarily the owner of the money, and the chart does not show who beneficially owned the money or who owned the company.[^82]

According to the FBI's report of the interview, the guard said a security organizer, Matthew Hellyer, had been retained by Maxwell's brother <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Kevin Maxwell</span> to arrange former British Army members as security, and that the organizer paid him $350 per day; he also said Maxwell gave him a business debit card in the LLC's name for needed items.[^40] The report does not trace the organizer's funding or show that the debit card paid the guard's wages.[^40]

The records do not establish who "Steve Marshall" was.[^76][^75][^84] According to a note of a July 14, 2020 call with the FBI, the United Kingdom phone number he gave came back to an individual whom a guard interviewed on the day of the arrest identified as someone who had previously worked as a guard for Maxwell; the note does not name that individual.[^76] The FBI's report of the witness interview, after recording the phone numbers the buyers gave, adds a database query identifying the user of a redacted cell phone as Marcus Wakefield.[^75] The numbers are redacted, so these records do not show whether that is the same number, or that he used the name.[^76][^75] The guard's interview report says that Wakefield provided security before him, and that before Wakefield "another person by the name of Steve (NFI) provided the security", NFI meaning no further information.[^84]

The defense said Maxwell married her spouse in 2016, but that passage does not name the spouse or give an exact date.[^48] In a December 2020 bail opposition, prosecutors described Maxwell’s marriage and bank forms on which both spouses listed themselves as single.[^37]

### Q4 — What do the records show about pre-arrest contact, surrender and the arrest?

Both sides' filings document pre-arrest contact between Maxwell's lawyers and federal prosecutors in the Southern District of New York (SDNY).[^49][^50] The defense said its lawyers communicated with the government in July, August, September and October 2019 and in January and March 2020; the government said the two sides communicated on multiple occasions between July and October 2019 and briefly on two more occasions, most recently in March 2020.[^49][^50] At the July 14, 2020 bail hearing, defense counsel said they had been in contact "eight to ten times in the past year", all to urge the government not to bring the case, and the prosecutor said the communications had been "minimal" and had not included information about her whereabouts.[^64][^65] Both sides place a contact on July 7, 2019: the defense said Maxwell "reached out" through counsel the day after Epstein's arrest, and the government said counsel made contact when the FBI tried to serve her with a subpoena and could not locate her.[^51][^50] At the hearing the prosecutor instead said the communications began when the government "served" her with a grand jury subpoena; the two government accounts differ on service, and they are not reconciled here.[^65][^50] An FBI email dated October 8, 2019, with the subject "Epstein briefing", contains the line "Ghislaine Maxwell: Attorney proffer on 10/8/19", and gives no other detail about it.[^52]

The parties dispute what that contact meant.[^53][^54][^50] In July 2020 the defense said that, had the government alerted counsel that she was about to be arrested, counsel would have arranged her "prompt, voluntary surrender".[^53] At the July 14 hearing the Court asked whether defense counsel had indicated to the government that, should there be an indictment, they were "seeking to arrange a voluntary surrender".[^66] Defense counsel answered that they were "urging them not to return an indictment and saying we were always available to speak", and, when the Court asked whether they were implying that they should be contacted for voluntary surrender, said "Yes, of course".[^66] The prosecutor later told the Court, "As defense counsel conceded, no offer along those lines was ever made", which is the government's characterization of that exchange.[^67] In December 2020 the defense said counsel would have provided her location had the government asked, and would have willingly arranged her self-surrender had the government reached out, and that counsel "were never given that chance".[^54] The government said that at no point did counsel disclose her location, offer to surrender her, or offer to bring her in to be interviewed, and it called the first two points "undisputed".[^50] These filings and the hearing state the dispute; they do not identify a specific surrender offer that the government accepted or refused.[^53][^54][^50][^66][^67]

A July 1, 2020 FBI affidavit said the FBI did not know Maxwell's current location.[^55] A premises affidavit said a cell-site simulator found the target phone at the Bradford property at approximately 7:36 AM on July 2, 2020, and that cell site data placed the phone in and around Bradford for at least the past 30 days; those are statements about a phone, not a day-by-day account of her movements.[^56] The FBI's public-affairs bulletin that day reported the arrest at approximately 8:30 in the morning, labelling the time EST, and said she was taken into custody "without incident".[^57] Prosecutors alleged that agents announced themselves and directed her to open the door, that she ignored that direction and tried to flee to another room, and that agents breached the door.[^58] The defense said she moved to a safe room under a security protocol after a guard, assuming the agents were press, warned her by radio; the government answered that the agents announced themselves and wore clothing identifying them as FBI.[^59][^60] The bulletin's short description does not settle that disagreement and does not say whether terms had been discussed earlier.[^57]

In its ruling at the July 14, 2020 hearing, which ordered her detained pending trial, the Court noted that she had stayed in contact with the government through counsel and that the government did not contest this, but said that staying in contact "means little if that was an effort to stave off indictment and she did not provide the government with her whereabouts", and that the circumstances of her arrest "may cast some doubt on the claim that she was not hiding from the government".[^68] The Court also said that even if she only wanted to hide from the press and public, "an assumption that the court does not share", her recent conduct "underscores her extraordinary capacity to evade detection".[^69] These are the Court's assessments in deciding bail, not findings of what happened before the arrest, and they do not settle the parties' dispute.[^68][^69]

**Limit of the search:** A search on October 31, 2026 of the Epstein Files Transparency Act (EFTA) and House Oversight Committee Production 01 held by this project read only the top-ranked results for each query, and a term scan covered the full text of the 2025 interview transcripts; these did not establish a 2019–20 surrender, immunity or safe-passage agreement with the Justice Department or the White House. **Limit of the hearing read:** The hearing transcript was located separately and is not part of that search; 59 of its 91 pages were read in full and the rest scanned by term, and it records the exchange above, not an identified surrender, immunity or safe-passage agreement.[^70] **Limit of these records:** Neither the arrest's outcome nor a limited search proves that negotiations did or did not occur.

The July 24–25, 2025 interview of Maxwell by Deputy Attorney General <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Todd Blanche</span> and its written proffer agreement came five years after the arrest and are separate, later records.[^61][^62] Blanche said at the start of the interview, "I'm not promising to do anything", and the agreement says "THIS IS NOT A COOPERATION AGREEMENT".[^61][^62]

## Open Questions

- **Question:** Which filed record names Maxwell’s spouse and gives the exact marriage date? **Consequence:** The bail passage in Q3 gives only the year 2016, so the page cannot date the marriage against the trust and property records.

- On what date did Good Boys open in U.S. theaters, and what, if anything, would that establish about the date of the alleged image overlay?

- **Question:** Who took the In-N-Out photograph, and what is the actual provenance chain that landed it at the NY Post?
- **Question:** Where was Maxwell between August and November 2019, and which New England locations did the government mean? **Consequence:** Q3 has attributed accounts only from December 2019, so the first months of the period have no documented itinerary.
- **Question:** What was said at the attorney proffer the FBI email records for October 8, 2019, and does any record outside the limited search in Q4 show surrender terms being discussed with the Justice Department or the White House? **Consequence:** Q4 documents contact and a dispute over surrender but not the content of any discussion, so the page can say neither that terms were discussed nor that they were not.
- **Question:** Who are the members or beneficial owner of Granite Reality LLC, and who funded the company? **Consequence:** The closing records show a wire from the company and a deposit listed under the real-estate agency. Who funded the company is not established by any record we could confirm. The LLC's beneficial owner is not established.
- **Question:** Why the on-the-nose reading material — <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Gup's CIA Book of Honor</span> — in the disputed August 2019 photograph?
- **Question:** What is the actual distance between Saffian's Encino law office and the Universal City In-N-Out, and does any source state it?
- **Question:** What contemporaneous July 2020 reporting, if any, describes how long Maxwell had been staying at the Bradford, NH property before her arrest?
- **Question:** Is there contemporaneous reporting establishing that Maxwell's whereabouts remained unknown to the public and the press across the entire interval between the two arrests, rather than only during the documented mid-August 2019 window? **Consequence:** Q4 records what the FBI swore on July 1, 2020 and the parties' accounts of counsel contact, but those do not measure what the public or the press knew.
- **Question:** Was the Aug. 12, 2019 In-N-Out photograph in fact the only publicly circulated image of Maxwell during this period, or were there other published sightings the public record reviewed here has not surfaced?
- **Question:** Did press and prosecutorial attention turn to Maxwell specifically, and before others in Epstein's circle, immediately after Epstein's July 6, 2019 arrest (rather than after his Aug. 10, 2019 death)? **Consequence:** Q4 records the parties' accounts of a July 7, 2019 contact and a subpoena, which do not settle comparative priority.
- **Question:** Has the NY Post ever, at any point after Aug. 19, 2019, publicly addressed how the In-N-Out photograph reached it or whether it was altered before publication?
