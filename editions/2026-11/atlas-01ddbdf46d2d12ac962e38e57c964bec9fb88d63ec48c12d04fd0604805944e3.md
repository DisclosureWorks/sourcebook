# Intelligence Authorization Act of 1991

**Reference.** *Reports what the sources say. States no conclusion of ours.*

Edition label: `2026-11`

> Not everything on this page is footnoted. Our citation check covers the main text, but not headings, the lists of related entries and sources, or open questions, which may state facts without a footnote. Check the footnoted text or the source before relying on them.

**Definition:** The Intelligence Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 1991 (Pub. L. No. 102-88, 105 Stat. 429) — enacted in 1991 after a four-year negotiation following the 1986–87 Iran-Contra Affair — revised the congressional intelligence-oversight framework established by the [Intelligence Oversight Act of 1980](atlas-57fbb56c98455e69a6a52b3b6c5a4fff423bc09eb5ac43701cbee952f9162362.md).[^18] The IAA defined "covert action" statutorily and revised and carried forward the notification framework, including the "fully and currently informed" standard and limited-notification mechanism, enacted in 1980.[^19]

## Overview

Per Haas (2023), the IAA is structurally classified as **prohibitive-procedural** in her two-dimensional typology of Covert Action laws: it added Presidential Finding requirements and congressional-notification requirements (procedural), and it imposed restrictions on the executive's freedom of action (prohibitive).[^1] The chambers initially passed their versions by voice vote;[^20] the House adopted the final conference report 419–4, and the Senate agreed by voice vote, after a four-year veto-and-renegotiation cycle in which the executive branch obtained substantive modifications to the original Senate Bill 2834 (101st Cong.).[^21]

## Historical significance

Per Haas (2023), each element of the IAA as passed was designed to address a particular abuse from the Iran-Contra Affair; the Covert Action provisions below illustrate this:[^22]

1. **No retroactive findings.**[^23] Reagan's December 1985 retroactive Finding for the November 1985 HAWK shipment had been a flagged abuse (Poindexter testified the President signed it on December 5, 1985; the President said he did not recall signing it and the White House asserted he never did); the IAA prohibits retroactive findings.[^2][^3][^34][^23]
2. **Findings must specify significant government participants and whether qualifying third-party participation is contemplated.**[^25] Iran-Contra had seen covert action funding by third countries (Saudi Arabia) and use of private citizens (those enlisted by <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Oliver North</span>);[^24] the IAA requires specified disclosures of significant participation.[^25]
3. **No findings authorizing action that violates federal law or the Constitution.**[^26]
4. **"Fully and currently" informed standard for the intelligence committees.** The 1980 Act required the Director of Central Intelligence and the heads of intelligence entities to keep the Senate Select Committee on Intelligence (SSCI) and the House Permanent Select Committee on Intelligence (HPSCI) "fully and currently informed"; the 1991 Act added that "the President shall ensure" the committees are kept fully and currently informed of U.S. intelligence activities, "including any significant anticipated intelligence activity," while keeping the DCI and agency-head reporting duties in new sections 502 and 503(b).[^28] See [Limited Notification as Inoculation](atlas-1ed123f0b1b3e7febfca57db46ded3bce48f88dcdc5a143069823dca13b83b91.md) for later limited-notification practice.
5. **Gang-of-Eight access mechanism.** The 1991 Act carried forward and elaborated the statutory limited-notification mechanism enacted in 1980, under which the president may, in extraordinary circumstances affecting vital U.S. national-security interests, limit access to a Covert Action finding to specified congressional leaders.[^29]

**The Bush 41 veto (1990).** Bush 41 vetoed Senate Bill 2834 over a "request" provision that defined U.S. government requests to third parties to undertake covert actions as themselves covert actions.[^30] The administration objected to the provision's vagueness and possible chilling effect on sensitive diplomacy.[^31] Per Haas, the committees removed the language Bush objected to and the IAA passed the following year (Haas describes passage by voice vote in both chambers, though the House agreed to the conference report by a yea-and-nay vote of 419 yeas to 4 nays), reflecting agreement that change was needed but disagreement over the details.[^35]

**Later controversy over limited notification.** Congressional Research Service (CRS) described the effect of Gang of Eight notifications on oversight as debated; Central Intelligence Agency (CIA) Director Panetta said that limiting notification of the National Security Agency (NSA) surveillance program, which was not a covert action program, to the Gang of Eight was inappropriate.[^4] See [Cumming, Alfred. *Sensitive Covert Action Notifications: Oversight Options for Congress*. CRS Report R40691. Congressional Research Service, January 29, 2010.](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/20100129_R40691_d794c3f875fd8ee612d681cb5e019bca6796161d.pdf) and [Limited Notification as Inoculation](atlas-1ed123f0b1b3e7febfca57db46ded3bce48f88dcdc5a143069823dca13b83b91.md) for the later debate.

## The IAA-1991 and NSDD-286 Procedures (Bruemmer’s Account)

Per [Bruemmer (1992)](https://openyls.law.yale.edu/entities/publication/f24d637a-5286-41ea-8701-e0ca63f4bc44) (n. 21) — written by <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Russell J. Bruemmer</span>, CIA General Counsel 1988–90 and Special Counsel to the DCI for Iran-Contra matters in 1987[^5] — Bruemmer writes: “The legislation was largely a codification of executive branch procedures established through National Security Decision Directive (NSDD) 286, issued by President Reagan in 1987.”[^6]

Bruemmer's contemporaneous personal analysis describes the legislation as largely codifying executive procedures.[^7]

The sequence ran from the Iran-Contra disclosures (November 1986)[^8] through the Tower Commission (February 1987) and [NSDD-286](atlas-6bf7a2b33962334bedf554eb4a6d2de104fc5a6765652be5fcae804f98326c20.md) (1987), which set executive procedures internally;[^9] Bruemmer characterizes the 1991 legislation as largely codifying the NSDD-286 procedures.[^10]

**Hughes-Ryan was repealed as part of the 1991 codification.**[^11] Per Bruemmer (1992) n. 21: "The Hughes-Ryan Amendment was explicitly repealed as part of the 1991 codification."[^11] The 1991 Act recodified the Presidential Finding requirement in the National Security Act rather than diffusing presidential responsibility for covert action.[^39]

**The constitutional question remained unresolved.**[^33] Bush's signing statement asserted that the Act could not detract from his claimed constitutional authority to withhold information.[^32] The IAA-1991 conference committee report explicitly acknowledged the question is unresolvable by Congress: "Congress cannot diminish by statute powers that are granted by the Constitution. Nor can either the legislative or executive branch authoritatively interpret the Constitution, which is the exclusive province of the judicial branch."[^33] This documents that the IAA-1991 did not resolve the constitutional question that McCormick & Smith (1987) warned could, if the President's claim succeeded, "strip the Intelligence Oversight Act of all force"; the conferees said only the judicial branch could authoritatively interpret the Constitution on it.[^12][^36]

**Haas’s classification and procedural continuity.** Haas (2023) places IAA-1991 in the **procedural-prohibitive** quadrant.[^13] Bruemmer describes executive procedural antecedents; Haas also recognizes continuity with existing practice while retaining her procedural-prohibitive classification.[^14][^13] Whether the codified procedures enabled or constrained conduct in practice requires evidence beyond their executive origin.

Did the 2008 Executive Order (EO) 12333 amendments and subsequent intelligence community (IC) reorganizations functionally restore CIA Covert Action authority that the IAA tried to constrain? **Consequence, on this page's reading:** If so, the IAA's prohibitive intent may persist nominally but not operationally. Haas (2023) mentions the 2008 EO 12333 amendments only as a clarification of the Director of National Intelligence (DNI)'s Covert Action role under the 2004 intelligence reform law (Intelligence Reform and Terrorism Prevention Act (IRTPA)) and does not address their effect on the 1991 safeguards.[^40]
