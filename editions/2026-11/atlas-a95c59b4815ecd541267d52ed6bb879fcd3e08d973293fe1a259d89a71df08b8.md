# Presidential Finding

Edition label: `2026-11`

> Not everything on this page is footnoted. Our citation check covers the main text, but not headings, the lists of related entries and sources, or open questions, which may state facts without a footnote. Check the footnoted text or the source before relying on them.

**Definition:** A "presidential finding" is the President's statutory determination authorizing a covert action.[^1] The [Hughes-Ryan Amendment](atlas-273f511a70ee65148b5de2dc0bfc90677a8dfef583ffe4ee71e10bacfc7b20c9.md) (1974) required the President to make the finding but did not specify a written, signed instrument;[^2] the subsequent [Intelligence Authorization Act of 1991](atlas-01ddbdf46d2d12ac962e38e57c964bec9fb88d63ec48c12d04fd0604805944e3.md) generally required a written finding signed by the President and specified its contents.[^3]

## Overview

The "finding" term of art emerged from the verb "finds" in the Hughes-Ryan statutory text: "the President finds that each such operation is important to the national security of the United States" ([Johnson 1989](https://doi.org/10.2307/2600495)).[^4] Under Hughes-Ryan, the finding was the legal precondition for spending appropriated funds on covert action; without a finding, no appropriated Central Intelligence Agency (CIA) funds could be expended on operations in foreign countries other than intelligence collection.[^5]

## Statutory framework

**Structural elements:**

- **Written form** — the default rule requires a written finding signed by the President;[^6] when immediate action is required and time does not permit advance writing, the statute requires a contemporaneous record of the President's decision and a written finding within 48 hours.[^7]
- **No retroactive findings** (added by IAA-1991) — Reagan's December 1985 retroactive authorization of the November 1985 Hawk shipment to Iran was a flagged Iran-Contra abuse;[^8] the IAA-1991 prohibits retroactive findings.[^9]
- **Specification of each U.S. Government department, agency, or entity authorized to fund or otherwise participate in any significant way, and of whether any third party is contemplated to fund or otherwise participate in any significant way** (added by IAA-1991) — addresses the Iran-Contra-era pattern of routing operations through National Security Council (NSC) staff (per [Johnson 1989](https://doi.org/10.2307/2600495)) and using foreign-government and private-citizen funds.[^10]
- **No findings authorizing illegal acts** (added by IAA-1991).[^11]
- **Notification to congressional intelligence committees** — initially "in a timely fashion" to six committees (three House, three Senate) under Hughes-Ryan, rising to eight once the Senate (1976) and House (1977) intelligence committees were added;[^12] reduced to two committees (the Senate Select Committee on Intelligence, SSCI, and the House Permanent Select Committee on Intelligence, HPSCI) under [Intelligence Oversight Act of 1980 (IOA-1980)](atlas-57fbb56c98455e69a6a52b3b6c5a4fff423bc09eb5ac43701cbee952f9162362.md);[^13] with IAA-1991 adding a duty on the President to ensure the committees are kept "fully and currently informed", a standard the 1980 act had already placed on the Director of Central Intelligence and agency heads.[^14] The 24-hour expectation for "timely fashion" was a congressional norm rather than a statutory requirement.[^15]
- **Gang-of-Eight limited-notice mechanism** (established by IOA-1980) — in extraordinary circumstances affecting vital U.S. national security interests, notice may be limited to the intelligence-committee leaders and House and Senate leaders who comprise the Gang of Eight;[^16] IAA-1991 did not originate this mechanism.[^17]

See [Limited Notification as Inoculation](atlas-1ed123f0b1b3e7febfca57db46ded3bce48f88dcdc5a143069823dca13b83b91.md) for the post-IAA-1991 evolution of limited-notification practice.[^18]

## Open Questions

- Are presidential findings litigated or judicially reviewed, or are they de facto immune from judicial scrutiny?
- Do the reported evasions of finding requirements and the disputed effects of limited notification establish that the finding system as a whole is procedurally elaborate but substantively vulnerable?[^19][^20]
- Were finding contents classified in order to limit committee discussion, as distinct from limits on access to findings or delays in notifying Congress?
- How should the IAA-1991’s new finding requirements be counted, given Haas’s grouping of three additional restrictions alongside the written-finding and notification provisions?[^21][^22][^23]
