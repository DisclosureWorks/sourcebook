# Money Laundering Cycle

Edition label: `2026-11`

**Definition:** the three-stage framework — placement, layering, integration —[^1] commonly used to describe how criminal proceeds are converted into apparently-legitimate assets.[^2] A typology taught in the Certified Anti-Money Laundering Specialist (CAMS) guide that can be used to consider named money-laundering methods across one or more stages.[^3]

## Overview

The cycle is a commonly used analytic lens on money-laundering operations.[^4] CAMS Study Guide v6.52 teaches that it is "common to think of money laundering as occurring in three stages" — placement, layering, and integration[^5] — which are sequential conceptually but can overlap in practice (pp. 5–6, "Three Stages of the Money Laundering Cycle").[^6]

**Stage One — Placement:** the physical disposal of cash or other assets derived from criminal activity into the financial system.[^7] Markers: cash deposits into financial accounts (structured below currency transaction report (CTR) thresholds),[^8] currency-denominated purchases of stored-value instruments,[^9] cross-border physical movement of cash or monetary instruments,[^10] deposits into cash-intensive businesses,[^11] foreign-exchange purchases with illicit funds.[^12]

**Stage Two — Layering:** the separation of illicit proceeds from their source by a series of financial transactions intended to disguise the audit trail.[^13] Markers: wire transfers across multiple jurisdictions,[^14] conversions between asset classes (cash → stocks → real estate),[^15] Shell Company passthrough flows,[^16] excessive journal entries between unrelated accounts without any apparent business purpose,[^17] multi-tier holding-company structures.[^18]

**Stage Three — Integration:** supplying apparent legitimacy to illicit wealth through re-entry into the economy as normal business or personal transactions.[^19] Markers: real-estate purchases[^20], high-value asset purchases (luxury vehicles, art, jewelry),[^21] business "investments",[^22] fictitious salary payments through criminally controlled companies, or consultancy fees paid to shell companies.[^23]

## Application to the Record

For Epstein-network targets, the cycle lens can distinguish separate patterns in the [New York State Department of Financial Services. *In the Matter of Deutsche Bank AG, Deutsche Bank AG New York Branch, and Deutsche Bank Trust Company of the Americas: Consent Order under New York Banking Law §§ 39 and 44*. July 6, 2020.](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2020/07/ea20200706_deutsche_bank_consent_order.pdf): outgoing payments to a Russian model and publicity agent,[^24] and to accounts of women with Eastern European surnames at a Russian bank, were monitored wire activity;[^25] separately, an attorney made 97 U.S. cash withdrawals of $7,500,[^26] and the bank said his pattern gave the appearance of structuring.[^27]

- **Question:** Does the <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Red Flag Indicators</span> anti-money laundering (AML) / Financial-Product domain's 12 sub-families each map to one or more of the three cycle stages as described (microstructuring purely placement; Shell Company patterns spanning layering and integration; real-estate patterns spanning placement and integration depending on the buy-side mechanics)?
- **Question:** Were the Little St. James / Great St. James United States Virgin Islands (USVI) real-estate purchases by Epstein-controlled entities in fact all-cash, making them integration-stage activity under the cycle framework?
- **Question:** Does cryptocurrency-native laundering specifically rely on chain-hopping as a layering technique, and is there a documented case tying it to the cycle framework?
- **Question:** Is residential real estate a predominant (rather than merely one attractive) integration-stage vehicle relative to commercial real estate?
- **Question:** Does customer due diligence / know-your-customer specifically detect placement-stage and layering-stage activity, as opposed to money laundering generally?
- **Question:** Does Beneficial Ownership transparency specifically detect layering-stage and integration-stage activity, as opposed to money laundering generally?
- **Question:** Should terrorist financing and criminal-proceeds money laundering be treated under one cycle umbrella on this page?
- **Question:** Are placement-stage cash deposits often (rather than sometimes) structured below currency-transaction-report (CTR) thresholds?
- **Question:** Is the absence of commercial logic a recognized test for identifying integration-stage business "investments"?
- **Question:** Do integration-stage salary or consulting fees typically flow back to the original criminal actor through an apparently-legitimate intermediary?
- **Question:** Is structuring the primary placement-stage typology?
- **Question:** Do cryptocurrency-native laundering operations (mixers, chain-hopping, DeFi protocols) compress placement and layering into a single mechanical step such that the three-stage framework no longer cleanly applies, or does crypto warrant a four-stage extension (e.g., on-ramp / mixing / off-ramp / integration)?
