# Iran-Contra Cover-Up

**Reference.** *Reports what the sources say. States no conclusion of ours.*

Edition label: `2026-11`

> Not everything on this page is footnoted. Our citation check covers the main text, but not headings, the lists of related entries and sources, or open questions, which may state facts without a footnote. Check the footnoted text or the source before relying on them.

**Definition:** The coordinated strategy by Reagan administration officials, beginning in October 1986, to conceal the <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Iran-Contra</span> operations from Congress and investigators; characterized by <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Walsh</span> as a deliberate plan to make <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">North</span>, <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Poindexter</span>, and <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">McFarlane</span> scapegoats while protecting Cabinet-level and presidential knowledge[^1]

## Overview

Walsh's Final Report quotes the "rogue operation" narrative, and the congressional committees documented the shredding of documents by North, Hall and Earl.[^2] Walsh's Final Report bounds the concerted effort to deceive Congress and the public as beginning with the testimony of Elliott Abrams and others in October 1986 and continuing through Caspar Weinberger's testimony on the last day of the congressional hearings in the summer of 1987;[^3] the later events this page also follows, the notes withheld from investigators and the December 24, 1992 pardons of six defendants[^25], are described in their own sections below.

## Timeline of the Cover-Up

**November 3, 1986**: Lebanese magazine Al-Shiraa published story about U.S. arms sales to Iran.[^4] <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Rafsanjani</span> confirmed November 4.[^42]

**November 12, 1986**: Poindexter falsely told congressional leaders there was no U.S. involvement in the 1985 Israeli arms sales to Iran.[^7]

**November 13, 1986**: Reagan gave public speech denying the Arms-for-Hostages (Iran Initiative) character of the initiative — "We did not, repeat did not, trade weapons or anything else for hostages."[^5] Walsh found this statement false.[^6]

**November 19, 1986**: Reagan press conference; said Israel was not involved — false.[^8]

**November 21, 1986**: <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Meese</span> launched his weekend "inquiry" — not a criminal investigation;[^9] interviews before the diversion memorandum was found were attended by a second Justice official and notes were taken, but afterward (except for North's interview) Meese met witnesses one-on-one with no notes taken; created no procedural barrier to document destruction.[^10]

**November 21–25, 1986**: North and <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Fawn Hall</span> shredded many records in the OEOB on November 21, and on November 25 Hall smuggled copies of the documents she had begun altering, together with PROFs printouts, out of the building in her clothing and boots.[^11] Justice Department attorneys reviewing NSC documents found an unshredded copy of the April 1986 diversion memorandum and alerted Meese.[^12] Meese learned of the memorandum on November 22, privately informed Reagan, Bush and Regan on November 24, and announced the diversion publicly on November 25.[^13]

**November 24, 1986**: White House Chief of Staff <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Donald Regan</span> authored a damage-control memorandum: "Blame must be put at NSC's door — rogue operation, going on without President's knowledge or sanction."[^14]

**November 25, 1986**: Meese announced the diversion at a press conference.[^15] Poindexter resigned; North fired.[^16]

**Late November 1986 — 1987**: McFarlane, Poindexter, and North became the publicly designated scapegoats.[^17] The administration's strategy cast their conduct as a "rogue operation," but North testified repeatedly that he believed President Reagan was aware of and approved his activities, though he could offer no direct proof of presidential knowledge and authorization.[^18]

**1987**: Congress compelled testimony from Poindexter (a Senate witness) and North (a House witness; immunity order June 15, 1987) under grants of use immunity,[^19] and their convictions were later reversed on immunity-taint grounds[^20].

**1987–1991**: In late 1991 the OIC discovered nearly 1,700 pages of <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Weinberger</span>'s handwritten notes from the 1985–1986 Iran/contra period.[^21] <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Charles Hill</span>, <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Shultz</span>'s executive assistant, withheld his own near-verbatim notes of Shultz's meetings and prepared false testimony for Shultz.[^22]

**September 1992**: Patty Presock discovered Bush's diary.[^23] White House Counsel <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Boyden Gray</span> did not notify Walsh OIC until December 1992.[^24]

**November 1992**: Bush's election defeat to Clinton.[^43]

**December 24, 1992**: Bush pardoned Weinberger, <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Clarridge</span>, <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Clair George</span>, <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Fiers</span>, <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Abrams</span>, and McFarlane — 12 days before the Weinberger trial.[^25]

## Walsh's "Scapegoat Strategy" Finding

Walsh's central cover-up finding: "the President's most senior advisers and the Cabinet members on the National Security Council participated in the strategy to make National Security staff members McFarlane, Poindexter and North the scapegoats whose sacrifice would protect the Reagan Administration in its final two years."[^26] This was not a finding that these three were innocent — North was convicted at trial (though reversed on appeal).[^27] The finding was that the cover-up strategy was designed to confine accountability to these three, shielding:[^26]

- **Reagan**: No criminal violation found[^28]
- **Bush**: Walsh found him "fully aware of the Iran arms sales" but obtained no evidence he knew of the diversion; his December 24, 1992 pardon of Weinberger pre-empted a trial at which defense counsel intended to call Bush as a witness[^29]
- **Weinberger**: Indicted but pardoned before trial[^44]
- **Shultz**: Walsh concluded his early testimony was "incorrect, if not false, in significant respects, and misleading, if literally true, in others"; Hill willfully withheld relevant notes[^30]
- **<span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Casey</span>**: Was terminally ill before OIC’s appointment; OIC undertook no formal investigation of Casey and could not obtain his account.[^31]
- **<span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Powell</span>**: Walsh found little evidence that Powell's early testimony regarding the 1985 shipments and Weinberger's notes was willfully false[^32]

## Document Suppression Pattern

Walsh reported that "large volumes of highly relevant, contemporaneously created documents were systematically and willfully withheld from investigators by several Reagan Administration officials."[^33]

- **North and Hall shredding (November 21, 1986)**: North and Hall shredded large volumes of NSC documents; a copy of the diversion memo survived and was found by Justice Department attorneys[^34]
- **Poindexter destroyed December 5, 1985 Finding**: Destroyed the finding to avoid political embarrassment, removing the document that explicitly described the operation as Arms-for-Hostages (Iran Initiative)[^35]
- **Weinberger's diary notes**: Discovered by OIC in late 1991[^36]
- **Hill notes**: "Willfully withheld" from OIC; false testimony prepared for Shultz[^37]
- **Bush diary**: Disclosed to OIC only in December 1992 (White House counsel told OIC on December 11, 1992), despite requests in 1987 and 1992 (Boyden Gray delay)[^38]
- **<span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Gregg</span> notes**: Gregg's own notes of his August 8, 1986 meeting with Rodriguez contradict in part his subsequent explanations of that meeting[^39]

## Open Questions

- Was there a coordinated plan agreed among Reagan, Bush, Regan, and Meese before Meese launched the "inquiry"? Or did the cover-up emerge from independent improvisation by each actor protecting their own interests?
- Did North know, when he selected more documents for shredding on November 22 (after the main North–Hall shredding of November 21), that Justice Department attorneys had found the diversion memorandum that day? Did North have independent warning?
- Were witnesses in Meese’s November 1986 inquiry placed under oath, and were the interviews recorded?
- Before Presock found Bush’s diary transcripts while inventorying the Bush family safes in September 1992,[^23] how long had the transcripts been there, and who had custody of them?
