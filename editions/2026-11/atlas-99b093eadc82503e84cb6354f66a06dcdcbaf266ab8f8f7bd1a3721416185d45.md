# Epstein Flight Department (JEGE)

**Reference.** *Reports what the sources say. States no conclusion of ours.*

Edition label: `2026-11`

> Not everything on this page is footnoted. Our citation check covers the main text, but not headings, the lists of related entries and sources, or open questions, which may state facts without a footnote. Check the footnoted text or the source before relying on them.

**Definition:** Jeffrey Epstein's in-house private-aviation operation[^1] — internally referenced as "JEGE"[^2] — staffed with full-time pilots[^3] who operated and maintained Epstein's personal aircraft fleet (a Gulfstream[^4], a Boeing 727[^5], and a helicopter[^6]).

## Overview

The flight department was the practical mechanism by which named passengers (including public-figure social contacts[^7]) traveled on flights recorded by airport codes such as PBI, TEB, SAF and TIST.[^8]

Personnel attested in the Morrison 2009 deposition include pilots <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Larry Visoski</span> and <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">David Rodgers</span>[^9] and aviation-maintenance technician Larry Morrison (the deponent in the 2009 deposition).[^10]

The department's record-keeping practices are themselves investigation-relevant: Morrison testified that there was no regulatory requirement to maintain passenger logs on private aircraft.[^11]

## How It Appears in This Research

- **Source of passenger evidence.**[^12] The <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Maxwell</span> trial relied on the flight department's pilot logbooks as Government Exhibit 662-RR.[^13]
