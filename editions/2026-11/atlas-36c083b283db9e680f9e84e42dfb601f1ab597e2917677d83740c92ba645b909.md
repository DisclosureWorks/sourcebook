**Definition:** WWII Office of Naval Intelligence program that enlisted organized-crime figures, including Lucky <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Luciano</span>'s dockworker and Mafia networks, in wartime waterfront security and intelligence work.[^1] New York later commuted Luciano's sentence in 1946[^2] after officials cited his wartime assistance.[^19]

## Overview

In 1942, following the accidental fire[^3] aboard *Normandie* at its New York pier[^20], amid widespread but unsubstantiated fears of sabotage, the Office of Naval Intelligence sought to secure the New York waterfront against German espionage and sabotage.[^3] It approached organized-crime figures who could reach Lucky Luciano[^4], then imprisoned at Dannemora.[^18]

The deal:

- Luciano's network supplied waterfront information and served as lookouts for sabotage and espionage.[^21]
- ONI sought Sicilian contacts and local intelligence for the Allied invasion of Sicily[^5]; Webb writes that what those contacts contributed has slipped into legend.[^6]

After the war:

- Governor Thomas E. Dewey (who had originally prosecuted Luciano) commuted his sentence in 1946.[^7]
- Luciano was deported to Italy rather than serving out his full term[^8]

## Significance in Whitney Webb's Argument

Operation Underworld is Whitney Webb's founding event for the entire historical arc she traces through to <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Epstein</span>:[^15]

1. **Webb treats it as a precedent for later intelligence relationships with criminal networks.**[^9] Her interpretation rests on the wartime cooperation and Luciano's later commutation;[^22] the cited material does not document a general policy of legal immunity and operational cover established at the highest levels.

2. **Webb presents it as the beginning of a new underworld built on cooperation between organized crime and American intelligence.**[^23]

3. **Webb writes that "the legacy of Operation Underworld has endured"** and calls it "the beginning of the creation of a new underworld entirely, one built on the cooperation, if not outright symbiosis, between organized crime and American intelligence" (p. 14)[^11]; no wider claim about post-war governance or CIA backing of the Mafia is made on this page.

## Later Interpretations

Operation Underworld is the starting point of Whitney Webb's argument linking WWII intelligence-mob collaboration to later covert networks and, ultimately, Epstein[^12]; this is her analytic framework rather than a documented chain of institutional continuity.

Webb treats the wartime cooperation as the formal establishment of a continuing, symbiotic relationship between organized crime and intelligence[^13]; that interpretation does not establish a direct evidentiary link to Epstein.
