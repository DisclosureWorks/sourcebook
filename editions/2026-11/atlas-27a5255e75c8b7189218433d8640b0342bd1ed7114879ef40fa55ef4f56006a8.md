# Non-Prosecution Agreement (Florida NPA)

**Reference.** *Reports what the sources say. States no conclusion of ours.*

Edition label: `2026-11`

> Not everything on this page is footnoted. Our citation check covers the main text, but not headings, the lists of related entries and sources, or open questions, which may state facts without a footnote. Check the footnoted text or the source before relying on them.

**Date:** Signed September 2007;[^1] <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Epstein</span> pled guilty in state court June 30, 2008[^2]  
**Parties:** Federal government (SDFL, U.S. Attorney <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Alex Acosta</span>) and <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Jeffrey Epstein</span>[^3]  
**Status:** Contested;[^4] subject of OPR review (2019);[^5] cited in Maxwell's appeal[^6]

## What It Was

A non-prosecution agreement negotiated by <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Alex Acosta</span>'s office that conditionally deferred federal prosecution while Epstein completed specified state-court terms.[^7] Under the NPA[^7]:

- Epstein pleaded to state charges[^8] (18 months;[^9] served approximately 13 months)[^10]
- Federal prosecution was conditionally deferred; after timely fulfillment, the agreement provided for no prosecution in the district and dismissal of any charges.[^11] According to the July 28, 2008 defense reply brief, the USAO retained the continuing right to indict (or to unseal already-existing sealed federal charges) throughout the deferral period if Epstein breached.[^12] Federal grand jury subpoenas were held "in abeyance unless and until the defendant violates any term of this agreement";[^13] evidence was preserved "inviolate."[^14] The NPA let the U.S. Attorney give breach notice within 90 days after Epstein's home confinement expired; OPR states he completed his sentence on July 21, 2010.[^15] 

- Epstein registered as a sex offender[^16]
- A monetary recovery mechanism was created for victims[^17]
- The <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Co-Conspirator Immunity Clause</span> clause provided that, if Epstein fulfilled the agreement, the United States would not institute charges in SDFL against any "potential co-conspirators," including four named women[^18]
- Judge Marra found a CVRA violation in 2019;[^19] the en banc Eleventh Circuit later disposed of the action without reaching that merits finding[^71]

## Origins and Negotiations

The case originated as a Palm Beach Police matter[^20] and came to the SDFL around mid-2006.[^21] Line prosecutor <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Maria Villafana</span> brought it to the office's attention.[^22] Key negotiation timeline:

- **July 2006**: Initial meeting;[^23] SDFL agreed to pursue the case[^24]
- Epstein's state-phase counsel included <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Alan Dershowitz</span>; in 2007 he added <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Jay Lefkowitz</span> and <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Ken Starr</span>[^25] — prompting <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Acosta</span> to invite CEOS (<span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Drew Oosterbaan</span>) to South Florida to brief Washington[^26]
- **Term sheet approved**: July 2007;[^27] started at 24 months, reduced to 18[^28]
- **NPA signed**: September 2007[^29]
- **State plea**: June 30, 2008[^30] — a nine-month delay due to Epstein's legal challenges[^31]
- **Work release**: the Palm Beach Sheriff's Office granted it on October 10, 2008 without notice to the USAO, which objected in writing on December 11, 2008;[^32] Acosta testified, "I think it was viewed as the office was hoodwinked"[^33]

## OPR Findings (November 2020)

Key findings:

- **No professional misconduct** for any of the five subjects (Acosta + three supervisors + the AUSA) on either the NPA negotiation[^34] or victim interaction tracks[^35]
- Acosta had "plenary authority" to resolve the case as he deemed appropriate;[^36] no clear standard required federal indictment[^37]
- OPR found no evidence Acosta acted from improper motive — no corruption, no favoritism based on Epstein's wealth or associations[^38]
- **Poor judgment on NPA:** Acosta's federalism reasoning was "too expansive"; federal interest view "too narrow"; state system understanding "too imperfect."[^39] He resolved before significant investigative steps were completed and agreed to "unusual and problematic terms" without required consideration[^40]
- **Poor judgment on victim notification:** Acosta's deferral of notification to the State Attorney left victims uninformed and created misimpression of intentional silencing[^41]
- The "breakfast meeting" (October 2007): OPR found no evidence it drove the NPA, which had been signed weeks earlier[^42]
- Acosta acknowledged during his OPR interview that he approved the NPA and accepted personal responsibility for it[^43]
- During work release, Epstein was permitted to spend up to 12 hours per day, six days per week, at the "Florida Science Foundation", an entity that did not exist until November 2007 and whose court-listed address was his attorney's West Palm Beach office.[^44]
- **Nine-month post-NPA battle:** After signing, Epstein's attorneys tried to invalidate the NPA by arguing no federal interest,[^45] escalating to the Criminal Division and ODAG.[^46] ODAG declined to intervene June 23, 2008.[^47] Epstein finally pled guilty June 30, 2008.[^48]

Barr said the NPA's denial of victims' allocution rights was "pretty aggressive" and, in his view, legally vulnerable.[^49]

## The Villafana Contradiction

Acosta characterized line prosecutor <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Maria Villafana</span> as supporting the negotiated resolution.[^50] OPR found the opposite — she drafted an 82-page prosecution memorandum, dated May 1, 2007, supporting a proposed 60-count indictment.[^51] Acosta acknowledged OPR's characterization was "much stronger" than his recollection.[^52]

## Interstate Travel Nexus

Acosta testified that, at the time, the office did not have evidence that Epstein traveled interstate with victims, and said he knew of no victim statements describing such transport.[^53] Managing attorney <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Andrew Lourie</span> thought the defense's argument that Epstein did not travel to Florida *with the purpose* of engaging in illicit sex with a minor was more persuasive than its other points, while rejecting its argument against the § 2422(b) charge.[^54]

## Co-Conspirator Immunity Clause

The NPA states that if Epstein successfully fulfilled the agreement, the United States would not institute charges against any "potential co-conspirators," including four named women;[^55] this wording was not an adjudication that they conspired.

## Impact on SDNY Prosecution (2019)

Barr vaguely recalled Justice Department discussions about bringing charges that would not run afoul of the NPA.[^56] The NPA did not bar SDNY prosecution.[^57] <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Ghislaine Maxwell</span> was subsequently indicted and convicted by SDNY;[^58] she has challenged that conviction partly based on NPA grounds.[^59]

### Maxwell § 2255 challenge (December 2025) — petition denied August 25, 2026

<span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Ghislaine Maxwell</span>'s [pro se § 2255 motion](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.nysd.654959/gov.uscourts.nysd.654959.5.0_1.pdf) (POINT FOUR) **directly challenges the SDFL-only geographic-limitation reading.**[^60] Maxwell argues that:[^61]

- The 2007 NPA's Co Conspirator Immunity clause was **not geographically limited** in its text;[^61]
- The clause was **separate from Epstein's own immunity** and Maxwell argues that she falls within the "potential co-conspirators" class;[^62]
- "From 2007-2019, the NPA was upheld by the Government" — framing the post-2019 SDNY prosecution as a unilateral breach;[^63]
- Per Maxwell's motion, the SDNY prosecution violated due process and the constitutional bar on arbitrary government action.[^64]

Maxwell's December 17, 2025 habeas petition renews an argument she previously made in her pretrial motion to dismiss and in her April 10, 2025 certiorari petition.[^65] Judge Engelmayer denied the § 2255 petition on August 25, 2026 (SDNY 1:25-cv-10468).[^66] Whether the NPA barred prosecution in the Southern District of New York was contested: Maxwell's collateral attack argued that it did, but the district court denied her petition on August 25, 2026.[^67] See <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Co-Conspirator Immunity Clause</span> for the parallel annotation on the immunity clause itself.

## Palm Beach Investigation Origins (Filthy Rich)

*Filthy Rich* traces the case from its origins: the Palm Beach PD investigation built by detective <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Joe Recarey</span> under Chief <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Michael Reiter</span>, received by State Attorney <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Barry Krischer</span>, and ultimately escalated directly to the SDFL by <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Reiter</span>.[^68] The book reports that Epstein served less than thirteen months of an 18-month sentence at the Palm Beach County Stockade, alone in his own cell and wing, with work release.[^69]

The book *Filthy Rich* presents Reiter's federal referral as a response to disagreement with <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Krischer</span>'s handling.[^70]

- Beyond *Filthy Rich*'s account of Reiter's disagreement with Krischer, what would establish whether Krischer's handling caused the federal referral or outcome?
- Does the co-conspirator immunity clause have remaining legal force affecting future prosecutions?
- How does OPR's account of the Florida Science Foundation work-release arrangement relate to its formal conclusions on prosecutorial conduct?
- What appellate proceedings, if any, followed the August 25, 2026 denial of Maxwell’s collateral challenge?
- How does OPR's official account of prosecutorial conduct on the NPA compare with other official investigations?
- Was the work release a technical breach of the NPA, and why wasn't it treated as one?
- What USAO closure action, if any, followed fulfillment of the NPA, and did any charges lapse administratively?
- Was Krischer's handling of the case influenced by Palm Beach social dynamics or communications with Epstein's lawyers?
