**Definition:** Bahamas-based offshore bank co-managed by Central Intelligence Agency (CIA) veteran Paul Helliwell and Chicago tax attorney Burton Kanter.[^1] Whitney Webb's account describes it as having simultaneously served as a CIA money-laundering vehicle,[^2] an organized-crime repository,[^3] and a tax shelter for wealthy crime-adjacent families,[^4] and reports that an Internal Revenue Service (IRS) probe of it was shut down and that a later Justice Department case was dropped, which a 1980 report said "appears" to have followed CIA pressure.[^5]

## Overview

Castle Bank & Trust was co-managed by Paul Helliwell and Burton Kanter and headquartered in the Bahamas.[^6] Whitney Webb presents Castle as a later example of the overlap among U.S. intelligence, organized crime, and offshore finance, rather than documenting it as an institutional successor.[^7]

## Multiple Simultaneous Functions

Webb's account presents Castle Bank as serving at least three functions:[^2][^3][^4]

**1. CIA Covert Operations Conduit**
According to Webb's account, Castle Bank was used to channel CIA funds for covert operations.[^8]

**2. Organized Crime Repository**
Webb reports that Castle maintained accounts for several people described by her sources as organized-crime figures; the cited material does not establish that Lansky or his network deposited funds there.[^9]

**3. Wealthy Family Tax Shelter — The <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Pritzker</span> Connection**
Webb reports that members of the Pritzker family were Castle Bank's largest depositors.[^10] Webb reports that the Pritzker family employed Burton Kanter as its tax attorney and that Kanter would boast connections to operatives from international criminal networks.[^11]

Thomas Pritzker appears in a 2004–2005 Palm Beach contact book in the Department of Justice production released by the House Oversight Committee ([DOJ-OGR-00022335](https://oversight.house.gov/release/oversight-committee-releases-epstein-records-provided-by-the-department-of-justice/)); its custody before that production is not established.[^12] Webb reports that both Nicholas and Thomas Pritzker appear in Epstein's black contact book with several numbers and addresses each.[^13] Webb treats that contact-book presence as one link between the Castle Bank history (1970s) and the Epstein network;[^13] she also traces an indirect chain through Samuel Zell, who was indicted with Burton Kanter in 1976 over money hidden in Castle Bank and who had ties to the Gouletas family, whom she links to Epstein.[^14]

## Operation Tradewinds — the IRS investigation and how it ended

According to the Supreme Court, the IRS began **Operation Trade Winds** in 1965 to investigate Americans' financial activities in the Bahamas, and investigators focused on Castle Bank in 1972.[^18] In *United States v. Payner*, the Supreme Court, recounting the District Court's findings, described the government's "flagrantly illegal" briefcase search but held that Payner could not suppress evidence unlawfully seized from a third party.[^19] Webb quotes a 1980 account saying CIA pressure "appears" to explain why the Justice Department dropped the broader Castle Bank case;[^21] that account is an attribution, not an adjudicated finding of CIA suppression.

Helliwell died in 1976.[^20]

## Historical Significance

Castle Bank is a case Whitney Webb uses to argue that CIA intelligence operations, organized crime, and wealthy establishment families could use overlapping financial infrastructure.[^15]

The contact-book entries[^12][^13] do not establish the nature of any relationship between Epstein and Nicholas or Thomas Pritzker.
