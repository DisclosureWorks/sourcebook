# CVRA Litigation — Doe v. United States

**Reference.** *Reports what the sources say. States no conclusion of ours.*

Edition label: `2026-11`

> Not everything on this page is footnoted. Our citation check covers the main text, but not headings, the lists of related entries and sources, or open questions, which may state facts without a footnote. Check the footnoted text or the source before relying on them.

**Definition:** The multi-year federal litigation in which <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Epstein</span> victims sued the U.S. government for violating the Crime Victims Rights Act (18 U.S.C. § 3771) by secretly negotiating the 2007 Florida NPA without notifying or consulting them, as required by law.[^1] *Doe v. United States*, Case No. 9:08-cv-80736-KAM (S.D. Fla.).[^2]

## Overview

The Crime Victims Rights Act (CVRA), enacted in 2004,[^3] guarantees federal crime victims specific rights, including the right to confer with prosecutors and be heard in proceedings.[^4] Epstein's victims — initially filing as "Jane Does" — argued that the SDFL's secret negotiation of the NPA, conducted without victim notification, violated their CVRA rights.[^5] The lawsuit was filed in 2008, shortly after the NPA was executed.[^6]

DOJ litigated against the victims for years across multiple attorney general tenures.[^7] Key moments:

- **2008:** Lawsuit filed by victims in SDFL (*Doe v. United States*, Case No. 9:08-cv-80736-KAM)[^8]
- **2009-2015 (<span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Holder</span>):** DOJ defended the NPA and litigated against victims' CVRA claims[^9]
- **2015-2017 (<span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Lynch</span>):** DOJ continued litigation against victims[^10]
- **2017-2018 (<span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Sessions</span>):** DOJ continued litigation[^11]
- **February 2019:** U.S. District Judge Kenneth A. Marra ruled that the Government **violated the CVRA** by entering into the NPA without conferring with the victims[^12] — the first court to so rule.[^13] He instructed DOJ and victims' attorneys to try to agree on a path forward.[^14]
- **September 16, 2019:** After Epstein's death, Judge Marra denied the victims' motion for remedies and closed the case, ruling that Epstein's death had made moot whether the Government's CVRA violation invalidated the NPA.[^15]
- **2021:** The 11th Circuit en banc (*In re Wild*, 994 F.3d 1244) denied Wild's mandamus petition, holding that the CVRA does not provide a private right of action for a freestanding suit outside an existing criminal proceeding.[^17] Per the en banc holding, because DOJ never filed federal charges against Epstein in the Southern District of Florida, no proceeding existed in which Wild could move to enforce CVRA rights.[^18] The en banc court did not reach the alleged CVRA violation's merits.[^49] See [In re Wild Doctrine](atlas-6ec21ac8a08d3f484e5216e1e7c3605cb8324203dcde68130a20ce5ff59b682d.md).
- **2022 (Feb):** Supreme Court denied certiorari on February 22, 2022 in *Wild v. United States District Court for the Southern District of Florida*, No. 21-351.[^19] See [Supreme Court of the United States. *Wild v. United States District Court for the Southern District of Florida*, No. 21-351, petition for writ of certiorari denied February 22, 2022 (reviewing *In re Wild*, 994 F.3d 1244 (11th Cir. 2021) (en banc)).](https://www.supremecourt.gov/search.aspx?filename=/docket/docketfiles/html/public/21-351.html).
- **2022–present:** Three successive legislative attempts to strengthen the rights of crime victims.[^20] H.R. 9444 (117th Cong.) died in committee.[^52] H.R. 5506 (119th Cong., 2025), the "Courtney Wild Reinforcing Crime Victims' Rights Act" — bipartisan (sponsor Wasserman Schultz; original cosponsor Burchett, September 18, 2025; Salazar and Frankel joined November 20, 2025; Ansari and Garcia joined May 13, 2026; Owens co-led the 118th-Congress H.R. 9858, not H.R. 5506)[^21] — is the current legislative vehicle, pending in HJC after Wasserman Schultz's November 20, 2025 Member Day presentation.[^22] See [*Courtney Wild Reinforcing Crime Victims' Rights Act*. H.R. 5506, 119th Cong., 1st sess. Introduced in the House by Rep. Debbie Wasserman Schultz, September 18, 2025.](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/BILLS-119hr5506ih/pdf/BILLS-119hr5506ih.pdf).
- **2025:** HOGR subpoenas former AGs Holder, Lynch and Sessions, citing DOJ's litigation against the victims[^23]

The CVRA litigation connects the 2007 NPA to the current HOGR investigation: Marra's 2019 district court ruling found a CVRA violation,[^24] the 11th Circuit en banc held that the statute supplies no private right of action for the freestanding pre-charge suit,[^25] and DOJ defended the NPA across multiple attorney general administrations.[^26] After the 2022 certiorari denial,[^19] H.R. 5506 is one of the bills introduced to change that framework.[^21]

## Bearing on the House investigation

HOGR's cover letters tied the Holder, Lynch, and Sessions subpoenas to DOJ's CVRA litigation;[^27] the Garland cover letter cited the Maxwell prosecution and non-release of files instead.[^28]

The <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Acosta</span> transcript sheds light on the pre-NPA period: Acosta read into the record OPR's finding that the co-conspirator provision expanded "with apparently little analysis" within the USAO,[^29] testified that he had "no recollection" of how the provision came to be,[^30] and stated his understanding that such a provision does not bind outside the Southern District of Florida.[^31] This testimony bears on the district court's 2019 analysis; the en banc Eleventh Circuit later disposed of the action without reaching the alleged violation's merits.[^32]

## The 2015 CVRA Amendment — DPA Coverage, NPA Gap

Congress amended the CVRA in 2015 to add a right to timely information about any plea bargain or deferred prosecution agreement; the amendment did not mention non-prosecution agreements or create a deferred-prosecution-agreement-specific right to be heard.[^33]

The 2015 text names deferred prosecution agreements but not non-prosecution agreements;[^34] *In re Wild* separately limits judicial enforcement through a freestanding pre-charge action.[^35]

## Boeing DPA (Fifth Circuit)

The Boeing deferred-prosecution-agreement (DPA) proceeding began in 2021 and reached the Fifth Circuit in 2023 on a petition for a writ of mandamus seeking CVRA remedies.[^53] The Fifth Circuit ruled the challenge "premature" in December 2023 — a distinct outcome from *In re Wild*'s private-right-of-action holding.[^50] In its 2023 disposition, the court denied mandamus relief without prejudice and expressed confidence that the district court would uphold victims' CVRA rights throughout the criminal proceedings.[^54]

the court to let their clients join — a party filing, not an institutional finding.[^39] The first two are
recorded here because counsel is characterising, and in the second instance directly quoting, the

plaintiffs' discovery** — what they asked the Government to produce, which is a different kind of fact
from what the Government held or what any named person did.[^45]

- Counsel's account of the court's standing ruling, at DOJ-OGR-00003738: "The Court has firmly

- Counsel quoting the court's DE 189 at 12 n.6 on the scope of the Government's equitable defense,

  after the fact and presentation of the non-prosecution agreement to them as a fait accompli."[^43]
- **Counsel's description of Request for Production No. 8**, at DOJ-OGR-00003746: "One clear example is

  the Government to give him a more favorable plea arrangement and/or non-prosecution agreement,

  while he used his significant social and political connections to lobby the Justice Department to
  obtain a more favorable plea deal."[^46]

  **What this establishes is counsel's description of the scope of a discovery request, not the
  conduct it sought to document.**
  <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Prince Andrew</span> and <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Alan Dershowitz</span> are named in
  **counsel's description of** the request, which describes it as seeking records of their efforts on
