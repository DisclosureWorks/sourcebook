# Degem Computers

**Type:** Company described in published accounts as Israeli intelligence cover and one of several distributors of Prosecutor’s Management Information System (PROMIS) software; those accounts do not establish its global primacy.[^1]

## Overview

Gordon Thomas and Martin Dillon, in *Robert Maxwell, Israel’s Superspy*, describe Degem as a company owned by <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Robert Maxwell</span> and used by Israeli intelligence, even before he bought it;[^2] Ari Ben-Menashe, in *Profits of War*, describes it as controlled by Israel’s military intelligence before Maxwell bought into it.[^3] Their accounts allege PROMIS distribution and operational cover;[^22] no corporate, software or operational primary record is supplied here to establish those functions independently.

## PROMIS Distribution

Ari Ben-Menashe[^4] and Whitney Webb, in *One Nation Under Blackmail*,[^5] allege that Degem sold PROMIS to foreign governments. In Ben-Menashe’s account, Maxwell’s $8 million fee concerns a separate $450 million Eastern Bloc money-transfer arrangement, and Maxwell’s additional 2% applies to gross arms-money transfers through his companies.[^6] Webb alleges a connection to Operation Condor;[^7] Ben-Menashe’s Guatemala figure is 20,000 government opponents killed or disappeared, not 20,000 disappearances alone.[^8] These causal claims and the count are not independently established by a primary investigation or victim dataset here.

## Latin America Operations

Gordon Thomas and Martin Dillon allege that, even before Maxwell bought it, Degem provided cover for Mossad intelligence collection in Latin America, including infiltration of **Sendero Luminoso** (Shining Path, Peru) and **Colombia’s ELN** (Ejército de Liberación Nacional).[^9] No primary operational record establishing those allegations is supplied here.

## Africa/Nairobi Operations

Thomas and Dillon allege that Degem provided cover for African operations through a Nairobi office and describe an attack on an Arafat emissary in **Brazzaville**.[^10] These are the authors’ allegations, without a primary investigative record here; the graphic Limpopo killing account is omitted.[^10]

## Kidon Travel Documents

Gordon Thomas and Martin Dillon allege that travel documents identified kidon personnel as Degem employees.[^11] This passage does not independently authenticate the documents or establish a broader comparison with other companies.
