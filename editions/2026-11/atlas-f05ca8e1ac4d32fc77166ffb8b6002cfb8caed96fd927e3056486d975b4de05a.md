# Nes Ziyyona Facility

**Definition:** In Victor Ostrovsky’s 1994 memoir, a facility at Nes Ziyyona is alleged to have been used for prisoner detention, human weapons testing and killings.[^1] These claims are not independently established by custody, laboratory or investigative records here.[^1]

## Background

The Israel Institute for Biological Research (IIBR) states that it was established as a government research institute in 1952 at Ness Ziona, where it remains.[^2] Its official history describes biological and chemical research; it does not substantiate the memoir’s allegations of human experimentation.[^2]

## Ostrovsky’s account of 1968–69

Ostrovsky writes that, while a military-police officer in 1968–69, he transported prisoners to a holding facility in Nes Ziyyona.[^3] He attributes the human-testing account to a source;[^3] no custody or laboratory record independently corroborates it here. Ostrovsky’s narrative alleges that Palestinian detainees were used to test whether weapons worked and how quickly they worked.[^4]

Ostrovsky reflects on his reaction in the same passage.[^5]

He writes that what frightens him in retrospect is “not the fact that it was taking place” but the calm with which he accepted it; this is his reflection on the account, not independent confirmation that testing occurred.[^6]

## The Soweto parallel

Ostrovsky alleges that a source described testing of “new infectious diseases and new medication” for Israeli drug manufacturers at an outpatient clinic for Baragwanath Hospital in Soweto, and writes that a Baltimore hospital served as a Mossad cut-out.[^7] The account is a memoir allegation, not an independently verified clinic or medical record.[^7]

For a related discussion of the memoir’s allegations concerning both locations, see <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Ostrovsky Nes Ziyyona Soweto Human Testing 1</span>.[^8]

## The 1991 Madrid Bush-plot tie-back

Ostrovsky alleges that three Palestinian detainees were to be framed in a plot against President <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">George H.W. Bush</span> in Madrid in 1991, and writes that he later heard from a source that the three, held in the Negev, were transferred to the Nes Ziyyona research facility and “terminated.”[^9] [Names withheld: alleged detainees.] Their identities and this account have not been independently corroborated here.[^9]

The memoir returns to Nes Ziyyona in its account of the alleged Madrid plot.[^10] That narrative connection does not establish a continuing detention or killing program, nor independently corroborate the earlier human-testing allegation.[^10]

## Significance

The memoir links alleged episodes at Nes Ziyyona and Soweto;[^11] it does not independently establish a continuous institutional program. Custody, medical and investigative records would be needed to test these claims.[^11][^12]

Allegations in this memoir should not be used to corroborate one another without independent evidence.

## Open Questions

- What independently authenticated records, if any, could test the memoir’s allegation of human experimentation at Nes Ziyyona? The official history of the Israel Institute for Biological Research (IIBR) does not establish that allegation.[^15]
- If the prisoner transport occurred as Ostrovsky describes, are surviving military-police records available to test his account? The memoir supplies no such record.[^16]
- Can any independently authenticated custody or investigative record test the alleged 1991 transfer and killing of the three unnamed detainees? The memoir does not supply such a record.[^17]
- Are there independently reviewable records identifying the facility described in the memoir and testing any connection to IIBR? A shared place name does not establish institutional responsibility.[^18]
