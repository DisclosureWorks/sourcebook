**Definition:** Gordon Thomas describes LAKAM as an Israeli defense ministry unit created in 1960 to obtain scientific data “by all means possible,” which he interprets as stealing or bribing people to provide material.[^1] It was distinct from Mossad and kept offices in New York, Washington, Boston, and Los Angeles.[^1]

## Overview

Gordon Thomas describes LAKAM, the Bureau of Scientific Liaison, as a defense ministry unit created in 1960 to obtain scientific data.[^2] Thomas writes that its "by all means possible" mandate in principle meant stealing or bribing people to provide material.[^2]

Thomas writes that Mossad saw LAKAM as the proverbial "new kid on the block": both <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Isser Harel</span> and <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Meir Amit</span> tried to have it closed down or absorbed into Mossad, while <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Shimon Peres</span>, Israel's deputy defense minister, stubbornly insisted that the defense ministry needed its own collecting agency.[^3]

Thomas writes that LAKAM slowly set up offices in **New York, Washington, Boston, and Los Angeles**, all key centers for cutting-edge science, and that its staff shipped boxes of technical journals to Israel every week, knowing the FBI was watching.[^4]

## Key Operations

### French Mirage Blueprints (1968)
Thomas writes that an engineer building the French Mirage IIIC fighter was discovered to have stolen over 200,000 blueprints and supplied LAKAM with data to build Mirage replicas.[^5] Thomas reports that the engineer received a four-and-a-half-year prison sentence.[^5] After this incident, LAKAM had "enjoyed little other success" until Rafi <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Eitan</span>'s appointment.[^5]

### Mossad economic espionage in the United States (Thomas’s account; not a LAKAM operation)
Thomas writes that a special Mossad unit code-named **Al** (Hebrew for "above") prowled through California's Silicon Valley and Boston's Route 128 for high-tech secrets, and that in a report to the Senate Intelligence Committee the CIA identified Israel as **one of six foreign countries with "a government-directed, orchestrated, clandestine effort to collect U.S. economic secrets."**[^6]

International warnings about Mossad, in Thomas’s account:[^7]

- **Germany (BfV):** Gordon Thomas alleges a technology-espionage warning.[^7] The correct agency name is Bundesamt für Verfassungsschutz, the Federal Office for the Protection of the Constitution (BfV), as given in its official brochure; the book’s “BFD” is an error.[^8] This warning and the French and British warnings below are Thomas’s account, not independently inspected government records.[^7]
- **France (DGSE):** issued caution after a Mossad agent was spotted near the Satellite Imagery Interpretative Center at Creil[^9]
- **UK (MI5):** briefed new PM Tony Blair on Mossad's efforts to obtain sensitive scientific and defense data in the United Kingdom[^10]

### Jonathan Pollard Operation (1984–1985)
Thomas describes the Pollard operation, run under Rafi Eitan's direction.[^11] Full details in <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Jonathan Pollard</span> and <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Rafi Eitan</span>.

Thomas records that Pollard was sentenced to life imprisonment and his wife to five years.[^12] The institutional background and operational descriptions above summarize Gordon Thomas’s *Gideon’s Spies*; that book’s handler and delivery allegations are not independently established by a primary record cited here, so the personal handler identifications and car-wash/fax details are withheld.[^13]

Thomas alleges that the material included arms-delivery data, missile locations, arsenal photographs and intelligence methods concerning the Middle East and South Africa.[^14] These are the book’s claims, not established here by a primary record.[^14]

## Institutional Context

Thomas describes LAKAM as a defense-ministry unit distinct from Mossad,[^15] but also writes that Eitan passed Pollard’s material to Mossad.[^16]

Gordon Thomas reconstructs conversations expressing distrust of U.S. policy.[^17] The named-person coordination allegation and purported verbatim exchange are omitted because no authenticated transcript or contemporaneous record is supplied here.

The preamble of introduced Senate Resolution 374 (116th Congress, 2019) records that the October 23, 1983 Beirut bombing killed **241 U.S. service members: 220 Marines, 18 sailors and three soldiers**.[^18] Gordon Thomas alleges that Mossad withheld advance intelligence, quotes Ostrovsky on the leadership’s reaction afterwards, and links the episode to Rafi Eitan’s later targeting of U.S. information.[^19] Neither the withholding allegation nor that causal interpretation is independently established here.

## Historical Context and Source Limitations

The historical descriptions on this page draw primarily on Thomas’s account, except where a separate official source is identified.[^13][^38] Its institutional narrative does not establish a connection between LAKAM and Jeffrey Epstein, and geographic or biographical comparisons do not independently demonstrate coordination. The following points concern limits on such comparisons:

1. **LAKAM's U.S. infrastructure:** Thomas describes offices in New York, Washington, Boston and Los Angeles, a sayanim network and Israeli consulate secure-fax lines.[^20] New York, one of those cities, is also where Epstein acquired a Manhattan mansion;[^43] that geographic overlap alone does not demonstrate coordination.
2. **South Africa:** Thomas alleges transfer of intelligence relating to South Africa.[^21] That account does not establish an Epstein connection or protection of a blackmail operation.
3. **Limits of inference:** Thomas’s description of LAKAM’s mandate and Rafi Eitan’s career does not establish that an intelligence-linked blackmail operation existed or inherited its practices.[^22] The related entries below identify roles described by Thomas; unverified personal handling and coordination allegations are withheld.[^22]
