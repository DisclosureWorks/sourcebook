# In re Wild Doctrine

**Reference.** *Reports what the sources say. States no conclusion of ours.*

Edition label: `2026-11`

> Not everything on this page is footnoted. Our citation check covers the main text, but not headings, the lists of related entries and sources, or open questions, which may state facts without a footnote. Check the footnoted text or the source before relying on them.

**Definition:** The doctrinal holding that the Crime Victims' Rights Act (CVRA, 18 U.S.C. § 3771) does **not** provide crime victims with a private right of action to enforce CVRA rights outside of a pre-existing criminal proceeding.[^9] The en banc Eleventh Circuit held that, because federal prosecutors never filed charges against Jeffrey Epstein in 2007 — instead negotiating the Non-Prosecution Agreement — there was no "proceeding" in which victims could invoke their statutory rights.[^10]

## Origin

*In re Wild*, 994 F.3d 1244 (11th Cir. 2021) (en banc).[^1] The 11th Circuit en banc denied Courtney Wild's petition for a writ of mandamus, rejecting the freestanding pre-charge enforcement action without reaching whether prosecutors violated the CVRA.[^11] The majority held that the CVRA does not authorize a standalone civil action outside a pre-existing criminal proceeding.[^12] The Supreme Court denied certiorari on February 22, 2022 (*Wild v. United States District Court for the Southern District of Florida*, No. 21-351), leaving the en banc holding as controlling circuit precedent.[^2]

## Effect on the 2007 Florida NPA

Per the en banc holding, and because the 11th Circuit covers the Southern District of Florida (where the NPA sits), no freestanding victim-initiated CVRA suit challenging the NPA can proceed in that circuit under present precedent, because no criminal proceeding against Epstein was ever filed there (the en banc court left open motions within a preexisting proceeding and administrative remedies).[^3] The en banc holding bars this freestanding CVRA action; the Supreme Court denied the petition for certiorari on February 22, 2022.[^3]

## Legislative Response

- **H.R. 4729** (116th Cong., 2019) — died in committee[^13]
- **H.R. 9444** (117th Cong., 2022) — died in committee[^14]
- **H.R. 9858** (118th Cong., 2024)[^15]
- **H.R. 5506** (119th Cong., introduced Sep 18, 2025) — "Courtney Wild Reinforcing Crime Victims’ Rights Act"; bipartisan (sponsor Wasserman Schultz with original cosponsor Burchett, Sep 18, 2025; Frankel and Salazar joined Nov 20, 2025; Ansari and Garcia joined May 13, 2026; Owens co-led the 118th-Congress H.R. 9858, not H.R. 5506); pending in HJC after Wasserman Schultz Nov 20, 2025 Member Day presentation[^4]

**Operative mechanism of H.R. 5506:** The bill would let a victim move for an order requesting that the Government seek annulment of a deferred prosecution agreement or non-prosecution agreement for failure to accord the reasonable right to confer.[^5] After a violation finding, the court would hold a hearing to evaluate whether the agreement should be annulled.[^6] The introduced bill contains no express retroactivity provision,[^16] so its text does not itself answer whether it would reach the 2007 NPA. Marra's 2019 district-court finding was not reached by the en banc Eleventh Circuit.[^7]

If H.R. 5506 is enacted, a victim seeking relief concerning an existing agreement could file the motion specified in proposed § 3771(d)(6), obtain a violation finding, and proceed to a hearing at which the court would evaluate annulment; whether that mechanism could apply to the 2007 NPA remains unresolved.[^8]
