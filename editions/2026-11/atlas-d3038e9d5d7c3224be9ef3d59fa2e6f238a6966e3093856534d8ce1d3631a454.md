# TRS (Temporary Relay Station)

**Definition:** In Ostrovsky’s glossary, a temporary relay station (TRS) extends the transmission range of listening devices; units may be planted or dropped from aircraft.[^2] The book separately describes an alleged Trojan disinformation device, without establishing that it is a TRS variant.[^1][^2] The glossary states:[^3]

Ostrovsky’s glossary describes TRS as temporary relay stations that can be planted or dropped from aircraft to extend listening-device signals toward a receiving and deciphering unit in Israel.[^3]

## Placement methods

Ostrovsky’s glossary lists two placement methods:[^4]

1. **Planted units** — the glossary lists ground placement of listening-device relays, without identifying the Trojan device as one of them.[^4]

2. **Air-droppable units** — the glossary lists placement by dropping relays from aircraft.[^5] It does not say that an aircraft supplies fabricated transmissions or that Ostrovsky was directed to disclose this variant.[^5]

## Functional architecture

Ostrovsky’s glossary describes the following relay functions and placement options:[^6]

- Relay signals from listening devices whose own transmission range is insufficient.[^7]
- Extend those signals toward a receiving and deciphering unit in Israel.[^8]
- Permit placement on the ground or by dropping a relay from an aircraft; the glossary does not describe aircraft supplying fabricated transmissions.[^9]

The separate Trojan passage describes an alleged location-based deception mechanism.[^10] It does not establish that TRS performs deception or that either device is immune to technical detection.[^10]

## Source and limitations

Neither the TRS glossary nor the January 1989 Brush-Fire passage establishes that TRS generated fabricated intercepts for that campaign. The glossary’s listening relays must be distinguished from the separate Trojan allegation.[^11][^12]
