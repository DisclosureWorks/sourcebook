# Intelligence Coordinating Group (ICG)

**Reference.** *Reports what the sources say. States no conclusion of ours.*

Edition label: `2026-11`

> Not everything on this page is footnoted. Our citation check covers the main text, but not headings, the lists of related entries and sources, or open questions, which may state facts without a footnote. Check the footnoted text or the source before relying on them.

**Definition:** Cabinet-level daily-meeting body established by <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">President Ford</span> on September 19, 1975 to coordinate the executive-branch's response to the <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Pike</span> and <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Church</span> congressional intelligence investigations.[^1] Functioned as the operational command center of what Olmsted (1996) characterizes as the Ford administration's "sophisticated counterattack" against the congressional investigators.[^2]

## Overview

The Intelligence Coordinating Group (ICG) was constituted on September 19, 1975, immediately following a September 18 memo from the Vice President (<span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Rockefeller</span>), <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Kissinger</span>, <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Schlesinger</span>, <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Buchen</span>, and <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Lynn</span> to Ford warning that "[t]here has been no coordinated political strategy to protect the intelligence community from continued disruption".[^3]

**Standing membership:**

- Secretary of State / National Security Adviser <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Henry Kissinger</span>[^4]
- Secretary of Defense <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">James Schlesinger</span> (later <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Donald Rumsfeld</span>)[^5]
- Attorney General <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Edward Levi</span>[^6]
- Office of Management and Budget (OMB) Director <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">James Lynn</span>[^7]
- Director of Central Intelligence <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">William Colby</span>[^8]
- Chief of Staff <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Donald Rumsfeld</span>[^9]
- Counsel to the President <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Phil Buchen</span>[^10]
- Counselor to the President <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Jack Marsh</span>[^11]
- Senior staff: <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Michael Raoul-Duval</span>[^12]

**Operational pattern:** daily meetings[^13]; centralized handling of all executive responses to the Pike and Church committees[^14]; coordinated speech and op-ed scheduling[^15]; "outside expert" assignments based on a "master plan" of organized public support[^16]; preparation of executive-branch reform alternatives to preempt congressional proposals.[^17]

## Historical significance

Olmsted (1996) describes the Intelligence Coordinating Group's activities in the executive response to the investigations:[^18]

1. **Drafted Ford's reform proposals**[^19] — the substantive recapture of the reform agenda[^20] that proposed expanded domestic-spying authority and criminal penalties for government employees' unauthorized disclosure of intelligence "sources and methods"[^21], and that, through Executive Order 11905 (February 1976), strengthened the DCI[^22] rather than the more restrictive statutory charter Congress had been pursuing.[^23]
2. **The Ford White House orchestrated and exploited the [Welch funeral](atlas-66cdd8443ab6cb5a8313b378422f4b1137d81259c4061ec94a815378b253978d.md)** (from the arrival of Welch's body in late December 1975 through his Arlington burial in early January 1976)[^24]; the cited source does not isolate the ICG's role.
3. **The Ford White House supported withholding publication of the [Pike Report](atlas-c3d52c8c6f8c310282d1e2a875c77e973f3b5d9dc42c95b0774b156429630a38.md)** (January 1976)[^25]; the cited source does not identify the ICG as coordinator of the campaign.

On this page's reading, the ICG may offer a useful comparison with Reagan's Office for Public Diplomacy for Latin America and the Caribbean, but establishing institutional continuity requires comparative evidence. The Tower Commission was an investigatory review board.[^26]
