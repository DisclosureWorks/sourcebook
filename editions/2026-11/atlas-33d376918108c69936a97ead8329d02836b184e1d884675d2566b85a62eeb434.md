**Definition:** Whitney Webb, drawing on Gordon Thomas and Martin Dillon,[^1] alleges that <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Robert Maxwell</span>’s Bulgarian companies participated in a technology-transfer operation called Neva and later came under Multi-Group. This account is not independently established by primary corporate or intelligence records here.[^2]

## Overview

Whitney Webb describes the following alleged connections, drawing on Gordon Thomas and Martin Dillon:[^3][^4]

- **Multi-Group:** Webb describes a later umbrella for companies associated with the alleged Neva operation.[^5][^6]
- **Operation Neva:** Webb describes an alleged Bulgarian intelligence operation to obtain Western technology for Eastern Bloc customers; the account relies on Thomas and Dillon.[^7]

## Mechanics

According to Webb, Bulgarian intelligence agents obtained technology, often under diplomatic cover, and Maxwell-owned companies re-engineered and resold it.[^8] She separately reports suspicions about Pergamon-Brassey’s involvement; this does not establish a general publishing-company cover mechanism.[^8]

## Organized Crime Intersection

Webb, drawing on Thomas and Dillon, portrays Multi-Group as an intersection of intelligence, organized crime and business.[^9] This is the authors’ interpretation, not an independently verified corporate or operational finding.[^9]

## Maxwell's Dual Role

Webb interprets the alleged Neva and PROMIS activities as evidence of Maxwell’s relationships with several intelligence services.[^10] This interpretation is not independently established by operational records here.[^10][^11]

## Significance
