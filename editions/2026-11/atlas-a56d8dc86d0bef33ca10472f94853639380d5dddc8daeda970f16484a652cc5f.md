# Interstate Travel Nexus

**Reference.** *Reports what the sources say. States no conclusion of ours.*

Edition label: `2026-11`

> Not everything on this page is footnoted. Our citation check covers the main text, but not headings, the lists of related entries and sources, or open questions, which may state facts without a footnote. Check the footnoted text or the source before relying on them.

**Definition:** 18 U.S.C. § 2423(a) covers knowingly transporting a minor in interstate or foreign commerce with the requisite intent;[^1] § 2423(b) covers the defendant's own travel with intent to engage in illicit sexual conduct and does not require traveling together with a minor.[^2]

## Overview

The interstate travel nexus was one of the legal debates inside the SDFL about whether to bring a federal case against <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Jeffrey Epstein</span> or leave the matter to state prosecution.[^3] The statute reaches both transporting a minor across state lines for the prohibited purpose (§ 2423(a)) and the defendant's own interstate travel with that intent (§ 2423(b)); the debate turned on what the evidence showed under each.[^4]

## How It Appears in This Research

<span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Alex Acosta</span>'s legal justification for the [Non-Prosecution Agreement (Florida NPA)](atlas-27a5255e75c8b7189218433d8640b0342bd1ed7114879ef40fa55ef4f56006a8.md):[^14] the Palm Beach victims were recruited locally;[^5] Epstein traveled TO Florida (from NYC, Paris, USVI),[^6] but the victims did not travel FROM Florida to another state.[^7] Acosta's account, as he reconstructed it in testimony, is that the evidence, as known in 2006, did not clearly establish the required nexus; but OPR records that by late August 2006 the line prosecutor and the FBI had obtained flight manifests, telephone messages and cell phone records that "show the communication and travel in interstate commerce" by Epstein and his associates.[^8]

The record differs from that framing in the following respects:

1. **<span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Villafana</span>'s correspondence**: The correspondence discussed whether a simple assault aboard an aircraft fell within federal special-aircraft jurisdiction;[^9] Acosta testified that he did not read the excerpt as showing that victims were transported across state lines for the purpose of sex; Rep. Stansbury contended that it showed the line prosecutor was aware women were being transported across state lines.[^10]

2. **<span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Lourie</span>'s acceptance of the defense argument**: <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Andrew Lourie</span>, the managing attorney of the Palm Beach office, believed the defense's argument that Epstein did not travel to Florida *with the purpose* of engaging in illicit sex with a minor was "more persuasive," according to the OPR report.[^11] OPR records that after the June 26, 2007 defense meeting the prevailing USAO view was that the defense presentation had not been persuasive.[^12]

3. **<span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Virginia Giuffre</span>'s statement**: Asked whether Giuffre's statement was already in the record, <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Acosta</span> answered "Not to my knowledge" and said he did not know which statement the questioner meant.[^13]

4. **SDNY 2019 indictment**: The indictment charged conspiracy to commit sex trafficking (18 U.S.C. § 371, with § 1591(a) and (b)(2) as the object offence) and sex trafficking of a minor (18 U.S.C. § 1591(a) and (b)(2)), alleged that Epstein sexually exploited and abused dozens of minor girls, and described three minor victims (Minor Victim-1 through Minor Victim-3) in detail; it did not resolve the earlier § 2423 transportation theory.[^15]

## Open Questions

- Given OPR’s account of the flight manifests, telephone messages, and cell phone records obtained by late August 2006,[^8] what did those records establish about each proposed transportation charge, and how did prosecutors assess them?
- Did Villafana's correspondence with <span class="link-unavailable" title="Not published in this edition">Lefkowitz</span> about 49 U.S.C. § 46506 reflect actual flight records in the file?
- How did the evidence and charging theories differ between the 2007 SDFL resolution and SDNY's 2019 § 1591 Sex Trafficking case?
